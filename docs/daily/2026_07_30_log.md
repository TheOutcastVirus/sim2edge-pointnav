## 2026-07-30 (latest) — PPO: reward, actor-critic, and the BC handoff

Phase 3 exists. The BC checkpoint plateaus at 59.8% held-out success with a 40%
collision ceiling: the imitation objective is uniform over timesteps, so long
open-space trajectories dominate it while pre-collision states carry 3.4x the
error. Imitation has no notion of reward — it learns the expert didn't
collide, never that collisions are bad. PPO optimizes return directly, so
those states become expensive rather than merely rare in the data.

**The reward is geodesic progress plus terminal outcomes.** Per control step:
`3.0 * (d_{t-1} - d_t) + 10 * reached - 10 * collided - 0.005 -
0.05 * ||Δa||²`, with `d` read from `Room.geodesic` — true metric path
length, the same field that sets episode difficulty and the SPL denominator.
Euclidean progress would pay the robot to drive into the concave face of an
obstacle. `geodesic()` leaves `INF` in blocked/unreachable cells, and the
reward is a *difference* of consecutive distances, so one step from a finite
cell into `INF` would pay a billion metres for grazing an obstacle.
`LayoutPool` now fills the field from the nearest reachable cell (the same
`distance_transform_edt` trick `flow_field` uses), padded with each room's
max. The per-step clamp is 0.5 m — deliberately far above one grid cell
(2.3 cm), since consecutive geodesic readings differ by whole 5 cm cells and
only the unclamped sum telescopes to real distance closed.

**The distribution lives before the tanh.** `NavActorCritic` wraps the
deployable `NavPolicy` and adds a diagonal Gaussian over the head's
pre-squash output, treating tanh + SI scaling as part of the environment. A
Gaussian over the bounded command would put mass outside the Create3's
envelope, and clipping back makes the density the importance ratio divides
by a fiction. Initial std is 0.15 of each limit — the execution-noise sigma
demonstrations were collected with.

The critic is a second 290k-parameter tower with no shared weights:
shared features would route the critic's initial noise straight through what
BC spent hours fitting. Refactoring `NavPolicy` to expose `pre_squash` and
`features` had one hard constraint — submodule names are checkpoint keys —
so the encoder was extracted into a `build_encoder` free function rather
than a shared base class, avoiding orphaning existing runs. Verified: both
`bc_stack_dagger_v2_e4_final/best.pt` and the legacy 1-frame `bc_v1/best.pt`
still load and produce identical outputs through the wrapper.

**Three handoff mechanisms, all load-bearing.** Naive BC-init + PPO reliably
destroys a good actor (random critic → noisy advantages → large random
updates to fitted weights). So: critic warmup with actor LR at exactly zero;
an annealed anchor to a frozen BC snapshot starting after warmup; a
conservative trust region (clip 0.1, actor LR 1e-4) with a KL early stop.
Warmup default is 25 iterations, not the 500 an earlier CPU implementation
used — at 65,536 transitions/iteration, 500 would burn 30M env steps before
any learning was permitted.

On-policy observations are corrupted in the environment, opposite the
collection-time rule (which exists to keep stored shards retunable) — on-policy
data is consumed once, so training on clean input would fine-tune against a
distribution neither BC nor evaluation uses.

Truncation is treated as termination in the advantage estimate, since
bootstrapping a timeout correctly needs the pre-reset observation the Direct
workflow doesn't return. Held-out eval puts timeouts under 1% against 40%
collision, so the bias is far below the effect being chased; `timeout_rate`
is logged every iteration so the assumption can be seen expiring.

Tested simulator-free: GAE against a closed form, boundary handling,
batch-wide advantage normalization, clip removing gradient on both signs of
advantage, a k3 KL estimator that cannot go negative, the actor provably
frozen during warmup, an anchor that demonstrably reduces drift, and a full
40-iteration loop on a toy reward a navigation policy cannot fake. PPO
checkpoints carry the actor under the same keys BC writes, so `eval_bc.py`
accepts them unchanged. Suite: **173 passed, 2 skipped**.

### Validated in Isaac, and two measurement lessons

Three runs, all exit 0, no PhysX capacity or CUDA errors.

A 6-iteration smoke run reported 0% success / 100% collision — the
*measurement* was wrong, not the code. 96 control steps is 4.8 s of
simulated time; a successful episode takes 200–550 steps, so only fast
failures can finish. This is the collector's yield bias, inverted, and the
runbook now warns against misreading it.

Pinning the actor (`--critic-warmup` ≥ `--iterations`) and running long
enough for episodes to complete reproduces the BC policy's own behaviour
through the PPO rollout with no learning involved:

```text
512 envs x 32 steps x 40 iterations, actor frozen, 2,900 completed episodes
success            55.03%      BC reference 58.40% at 1x noise
collision          44.83%                   41.02%
timeout             0.14%                    0.59%
mean episode      201 steps (10.1 s)
max |approx_kl|   1.6e-07      over the whole run
throughput        13,205 steps/s
```

55.0% against a 58.4% deterministic baseline is the correct relationship —
the gap is what sampling at `init_std = 0.15` costs. Observation assembly,
live corruption, the `tanh(raw)` convention, termination and reward are
therefore wired correctly; `approx_kl` at 1.6e-07 confirms the warmup
freeze holds live, not just in the unit test. Per-iteration success swings
±7 points because each iteration completes only ~75 episodes.

**Raw value loss cannot be read as critic health, in either direction** — it
was misread twice (a dip taken as learning, a flat stretch taken as a
plateau) before being replaced:

```text
iter   ev      value_loss   completed episodes
   2  0.024      0.9248       16
   4  0.221      2.5260       66
   6  0.554      2.4988       84
   8  0.759      2.1561       88
```

The critic improved monotonically while its loss more than doubled, because
targets grew as terminal rewards began landing. `explained_variance` is now
logged before each update; it also confirms the 25-iteration warmup default
has margin (0.76 reached on 16k transitions/iteration, against 65k in
production).

cares about. Against the original exit criterion (95% success, <2% collision
at deployment noise): success met at 94.9%, collision not at 4.7%.

## 2026-07-30 — Temporal BC and second DAgger iteration

The closed-loop plateau was broken without adding a recurrent deployment
state: the policy now receives four chronological lidar scans and four
previous commands (150 ms of history at 20 Hz) plus the current goal,
assembled from existing sequential raw shards. Episode starts repeat their
first frame; live evaluator/collector reset the same FIFO per environment.
Raw dataset fingerprint is preserved; checkpoints carry a separate
observation-layout fingerprint. The CNN treats the four scans as input
channels: 290,610 parameters, a 731-element flat input, same bounded action
output.

The first stacked run on `data/bc_dagger_v1` improved held-out clean success
36.5%→38.9%, not a useful stopping point. A second DAgger rollout used that
policy to visit 16,460 episodes, adding 1,839,025 oracle-labelled
transitions to `data/bc_dagger_v2`:

```text
16460 episodes, 1839025 steps
kept, reached the goal   : 5292
kept, truncated at graze : 11168
kept, ran out of clock   : 0
```

Two passes over the enlarged corpus regressed success to 34.8% — underfitting
from a fixed epoch budget against harder on-policy states, not overfitting.
Four passes reduced clean validation loss 0.01094→0.00797 and produced the
first large closed-loop gain (`runs/bc_stack_dagger_v2_e4_final/best.pt`):

## 2026-07-30 — Diverse BC closed-loop diagnosis and next steps

The eight-epoch `bc_diverse_v1` run did **not** overfit conventionally —
train/validation losses continued down together, noisy validation finished
essentially equal to training. But closed-loop eval on the original
8,192-layout pool was no better than on a completely new layout seed (35.4%
vs 36.7% clean success, both dominated by collisions) — rules out layout
memorization as the primary failure.

```text
epoch 8/8
train_loss                  0.0057258
validation_clean_loss       0.0055327
validation_noisy_loss       0.0057666

clean closed loop           success     collision    timeout
collection pool, seed 13579   35.35%       64.65%       0.00%
new pool, seed 10000000       36.72%       62.30%       0.98%
```

Noise isn't the explanation either (36.7%→36.5%→35.5% across 0x/1x/2x);
SPL tracked success closely, so successful episodes followed efficient
routes while failures left the expert distribution and collided.

An offline diagnostic over 1,000,671 clean samples from five training shards
found error strongly concentrated in safety-relevant states despite small
whole-corpus MSE:

```text
group                  samples   v normalized MSE   v MAE       signed v error
all                  1,000,671       0.007662       0.01337 m/s  -0.00121 m/s
collision, last 20      10,880       0.026274       0.02966 m/s  +0.01920 m/s
collision, last 5        2,720       0.023985       0.02879 m/s  +0.01349 m/s
minimum range <0.30 m   56,344       0.014774       0.02007 m/s  +0.00249 m/s
```

The final 20 pre-collision steps have 3.4x the normalized velocity MSE and
2.2x the physical MAE of an average sample; positive signed error means the
policy drives too fast exactly where it needs margin. Uniform timestep loss
hides this because long, easy, open-space trajectories dominate the
15.8M-sample corpus.

### Recommended next experiments

1. Safety-aware weighting: 5x on the final 20 samples of collision episodes,
   3x when minimum lidar range < 0.30 m, asymmetric penalty for
   over-predicting forward speed near obstacles. Episode-balanced sampling
   over allowing long successful episodes to set the objective.
2. Before recollecting, test a proximity-based forward-speed cap — a cheap
   causal check of the corner-overdrive failure, useful as a deployment
   safety layer regardless.
3. DAgger: current policy drives, oracle labels every visited state,
   oversampled so it isn't drowned by the existing 15.8M transitions. Random
   DART perturbations don't reproduce the policy's systematic closed-loop
   drift.
4. If DAgger plateaus, change what the policy sees. The teacher sees a
   privileged global flow field while the feed-forward policy sees only one
   lidar scan, the goal vector, and the previous command — occluded topology
   can make the teacher action unidentifiable from the policy observation.
   Prefer a planner-provided local waypoint where a map is available; for
   mapless navigation, use a recurrent policy trained on sequences.

The nearly equal train/validation curves and equally poor train/new-layout
rollouts show the objective and state distribution, not dataset size, are
the bottleneck — don't spend the next run only adding demonstrations or
epochs. Future checkpoint selection must include closed-loop success, not
just offline validation loss.

## 2026-07-30 — Behaviour-cloning trainer and closed-loop evaluation

Phase 2 trains directly from `data/overnight_v2`: 623 shards, 124,543,727
samples, 104 GB compressed. Concatenating would waste the 128 GB host memory
and make every epoch wait on a monolithic load, so training streams one
shard on the GPU while four background workers inflate an ordered,
eight-shard queue into RAM (bounded ~2.4 GB). Four whole shards are held
out — the collector emits only whole episodes, so no timestep crosses that
boundary.

The first production run exposed an input bottleneck: ~1 CPU core, ~126 MB/s
NVMe reads, only 1.5 GB of the 4090 resident. Loading all raw data isn't an
option (expands to ~185 GB) but parallel zlib decoding is — eight loads took
1.135 s serially vs 0.296 s with four threads (3.84x). Delivery stays
ordered, batch seeds unchanged, loader-only options ignored by resume
compatibility checks. Epoch metrics now report seconds lost waiting for
input.

The policy is a 290,034-parameter lidar CNN plus small goal/action encoders,
flat input exactly `OBS_DIM`, emitting physical `(v, omega)` through bounded
in-graph scaling. A `torch.export` test pins the deployment signature before
expensive training.

4090 defaults: batch 8192, BF16 autocast, `torch.compile`, fused AdamW, TF32,
two corpus passes. Loss is MSE in normalized action coordinates with 1.5x
angular weight, so SI unit magnitudes can't silently dominate the objective.
Checkpoints include spec fingerprint, dataset manifest, optimizer, scheduler,
shard cursor; resume rejects changes instead of creating a hybrid run.

The Isaac evaluator reprocesses raw scans through the same corruption/binning
as the loader; every environment contributes exactly one episode, so
re-seeded 0x/1x/2x tiers share the identical layout vector. Reports SR, SPL,
collision, timeout, time-to-goal, jerk, inference latency.

Two integration failures caught at bring-up: persisted NumPy 2 scalar
metrics couldn't load in Isaac's older NumPy, so checkpoint metadata is now
restricted to Python scalars; the paired-layout assertion was exercised
across two noise tiers. Final verification: **125 passed, 2 skipped**, a
compiled BF16 real-shard smoke test, and a successful closed-loop rollout.

### The entire pre-fix collection is unusable

An independent check of `data/overnight/bc_150.npz` compared each physical
command with the pose delta it produced over ~998k transitions:

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---:|
| `corr(commanded omega, achieved omega)` | +0.021 |
| `corr(commanded v, achieved speed)` | -0.030 |
| mean commanded `\|omega\|` | 1.52 rad/s |
| mean achieved `\|omega\|` | 0.19 rad/s |

Median achieved yaw rate was effectively zero in every command bin (386k
samples near -1.9 rad/s, 325k near +1.9 rad/s). Commands of 0.4–0.5 m/s
produced median speed 0.020 m/s, below the 0.068 m/s median while commanding
zero. One 899-step episode held `(v, omega) = (0.375, -1.31)` for 45 s but
moved only 7 cm and did not rotate.

Oracle labels were valid (the oracle reads the flow field) but the state
distribution was not — visited states came from a base drifting
independently of its command. All root-level `data/bc_*.npz` and all of
`data/overnight/` predate the fix and must never be used for training.
`data/overnight/bc_003.npz` is also a corrupt 170-byte partial ZIP.

### Holding the wheel-drop joints was necessary but not sufficient

The imported wheel-drop suspension joints, previously unactuated, are now
held at nominal position with high stiffness/damping, eliminating free
suspension that unloaded wheels and caused lateral slip. Straight commands
then tracked, but opposing wheel speeds still scrubbed the imported
finite-width cylinder contacts instead of turning the base — not a tire
model, and no actuator gain tuning gives a reliable differential-drive
response.

`Sim2EdgeNavEnv` now integrates `(v, omega)` into an independent planar pose
target at the 200 Hz physics rate and writes the corresponding root pose and
velocity directly. Wheel joints still animate from differential-drive
inverse kinematics. Reset synchronizes the planar target with the new
episode pose.

### Collection now refuses to start without a drive-response check

`collect_bc.py` runs a mandatory preflight before allocating the recorder or
writing a sample: turn in place `(v, omega) = (0, 1.0)` and drive straight
`(0.3, 0)`, 50 ticks each. Achieved response must be within 20% of the
commanded axis, with cross-axis limits. `--check` uses the same assertion.
Real Isaac measured exactly 1.000 rad/s and 0.300 m/s. Tests: **116 passed,
2 skipped**.
