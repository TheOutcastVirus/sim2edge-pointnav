## 2026-07-30 (latest) — Temporal BC and second DAgger iteration

The closed-loop plateau was broken without adding a recurrent deployment
state. The policy now receives four chronological lidar scans and four previous
commands (150 ms of history at 20 Hz), plus the current goal. History is
assembled from the existing sequential raw shards; episode starts repeat their
first frame, and the live evaluator/collector reset the same FIFO per
environment. This preserves the raw dataset fingerprint while checkpoints carry
a separate observation-layout fingerprint. The CNN treats the four scans as
input channels and remains small and exportable: 290,610 parameters, a
731-element flat input, and the same bounded physical action output.

The first stacked run on `data/bc_dagger_v1` improved held-out clean success
from 36.5% to 38.9%, but that was not a useful stopping point. A second DAgger
rollout used that stacked policy to visit 16,460 episodes and added 1,839,025
oracle-labelled transitions to `data/bc_dagger_v2`:

```text
16460 episodes, 1839025 steps
kept, reached the goal   : 5292
kept, truncated at graze : 11168
kept, ran out of clock   : 0
```

Two training passes over the enlarged corpus regressed success to 34.8% while
train and validation loss stayed equally high. This was underfitting from
holding the epoch budget fixed after adding harder on-policy states, not
overfitting. Four passes reduced clean validation loss from 0.01094 to 0.00797
and produced the first large closed-loop gain. The final, post-test checkpoint
is `runs/bc_stack_dagger_v2_e4_final/best.pt`.

```text
[BC] train=94 shards / 18,585,289 samples
[BC] 2,228 updates/epoch, 8,912 total
[BC] epoch summary  train_loss=0.00764
                    validation_clean_loss=0.00797
                    validation_noisy_loss=0.00816

held-out seed 10000000   success   collision   timeout    SPL
noise 0x                  59.77%      40.04%      0.20%   0.596
noise 1x                  58.40%      41.02%      0.59%   0.582
noise 2x                  55.47%      43.95%      0.59%   0.553

training-layout seed 13579, clean
                          62.11%      37.30%      0.59%   0.619
```

The clean held-out gain is +23.3 percentage points over the 36.5% one-frame
DAgger baseline, while collision falls by 21.3 points. Training-layout and
held-out results are close (62.1% versus 59.8%), so this is not layout
memorization. Robustness degrades gradually rather than collapsing at 2x
noise. This is a good checkpoint to stop broad architecture churn and move to
deployment validation; recurrence remains justified only if the remaining
40% collision ceiling is unacceptable for the product requirement.

Final verification: the representative new DAgger shard passed every
structural/label/outcome/history check, and the complete suite passed **140
tests with 2 skipped**. Durable evaluation reports are under
`runs/bc_stack_dagger_v2_e4_final/`.

## 2026-07-30 — Diverse BC closed-loop diagnosis and next steps

The eight-epoch `bc_diverse_v1` run did **not** overfit in the conventional
sense. Training and validation losses continued down together, and noisy
validation finished essentially equal to training. More importantly, a
closed-loop evaluation on the original 8,192-layout collection pool was no
better than evaluation on a completely new layout seed: clean success was
35.4% on the collection pool and 36.7% on the new pool. Both were dominated by
collisions. This rules out layout memorization as the primary failure.

Persisted training and evaluation log excerpts:

```text
epoch 8/8
train_loss                  0.0057258
validation_clean_loss       0.0055327
validation_noisy_loss       0.0057666

clean closed loop           success     collision    timeout
collection pool, seed 13579   35.35%       64.65%       0.00%
new pool, seed 10000000       36.72%       62.30%       0.98%
```

Noise is not the explanation: new-pool success only moved from 36.7% at 0x
noise to 36.5% at 1x and 35.5% at 2x. SPL was almost identical to success, so
successful episodes followed efficient routes; failures instead left the
expert distribution and collided.

An offline diagnostic ran the final policy over 1,000,671 clean samples from
five spread-out training shards. Error was strongly concentrated in the states
that matter for safety, even though uniform whole-corpus MSE looked small:

```text
group                  samples   v normalized MSE   v MAE       signed v error
all                  1,000,671       0.007662       0.01337 m/s  -0.00121 m/s
collision, last 20      10,880       0.026274       0.02966 m/s  +0.01920 m/s
collision, last 5        2,720       0.023985       0.02879 m/s  +0.01349 m/s
minimum range <0.30 m   56,344       0.014774       0.02007 m/s  +0.00249 m/s
```

The final 20 pre-collision steps have 3.4x the normalized linear-velocity MSE
and 2.2x the physical MAE of an average sample. The positive signed error means
the learned policy drives too fast precisely where it needs margin. Uniform
timestep loss hides this because long, easy, open-space trajectories dominate
the 15.8M-sample corpus.

### Recommended next experiments

1. Train from the existing corpus with safety-aware weighting: start with 5x
   weight on the final 20 samples of collision episodes, 3x when minimum lidar
   range is below 0.30 m, and an asymmetric penalty for over-predicting forward
   speed near obstacles. Episode-balanced sampling is preferable to allowing
   long successful episodes to set the objective.
2. Before recollecting, evaluate a proximity-based forward-speed cap. This is
   a cheap causal test of the measured corner-overdrive failure and is useful as
   a deployment safety layer even if learning later makes it rarely active.
3. Collect DAgger data with the current policy driving and the oracle labelling
   every visited state. Oversample or explicitly mix this data so it is not
   drowned by the existing 15.8M transitions. Random DART perturbations produce
   a tube around the expert, but they do not reproduce the policy's systematic
   closed-loop drift.
4. If DAgger plateaus, change the information available to the policy. The
   teacher sees a privileged global flow field, while the feed-forward policy
   sees only one lidar scan, the final-goal vector and the previous command.
   Occluded topology can therefore make the teacher action unidentifiable from
   the policy observation. Prefer a planner-provided local waypoint when a map
   is available; for truly mapless navigation, use a recurrent policy trained
   on sequences.

Do not spend the next run only adding ordinary oracle demonstrations or epochs.
The nearly equal train/validation curves and equally poor train/new-layout
rollouts show that the present objective and state distribution, rather than
dataset size alone, are the bottleneck. Future checkpoint selection must also
include closed-loop success instead of relying only on offline validation loss.

## 2026-07-30 — Behaviour-cloning trainer and closed-loop evaluation

Phase 2 now trains directly from `data/overnight_v2`: 623 shards, 124,543,727
samples and 104 GB compressed. Concatenating that corpus would waste the 128 GB
host memory and make every epoch wait on a monolithic load, so training streams
one shard on the GPU while four background workers inflate an ordered,
eight-shard queue into RAM. The queue is bounded at about 2.4 GB. Four entire
shards are held out; the collector emits only whole episodes, so no adjacent
timestep can cross that boundary.

The first production run exposed the input bottleneck: the process used roughly
one CPU core, NVMe reads were about 126 MB/s, and only 1.5 GB of the 4090 was
resident while utilization fell between bursts. Loading all raw data is not an
option--it expands to roughly 185 GB--but parallel zlib decoding is. On a cached
real shard, eight loads took 1.135 s serially and 0.296 s with four threads, a
3.84x decompression speedup. Delivery remains ordered, batch seeds are unchanged,
and loader-only options are ignored by resume compatibility checks. Epoch
metrics now state how many seconds were actually lost waiting for input.

The policy is a 290,034-parameter lidar CNN plus small goal/action encoders. Its
single flat input is exactly `OBS_DIM`, and its graph emits physical `(v, omega)`
through bounded in-graph scaling. A `torch.export` test pins that deployment
signature before expensive training begins.

4090 defaults are batch 8192, BF16 autocast, `torch.compile`, fused AdamW, TF32
and two corpus passes. Loss is MSE in normalized action coordinates, with a 1.5x
angular weight, so SI unit magnitudes cannot silently choose the objective.
Checkpoints include the spec fingerprint, exact dataset manifest, optimizer,
scheduler and shard cursor; resume rejects changes instead of creating a hybrid
run.

The Isaac evaluator reprocesses raw scans through the same corruption and
binning functions as the loader. Every parallel environment contributes exactly
one episode, allowing re-seeded 0x/1x/2x noise tiers to use the identical layout
vector. It reports SR, SPL, collision, timeout, time-to-goal, jerk and inference
latency.

Two integration failures were caught during bring-up: persisted NumPy 2 scalar
metrics could not be loaded in Isaac's older NumPy environment, so checkpoint
metadata is now restricted to Python scalars; and the paired-layout assertion
was exercised across two noise tiers. Final verification: **125 passed, 2
skipped**, a compiled BF16 real-shard train/validation smoke, and a successful
closed-loop Isaac rollout.

## 2026-07-30 — Invalidated collection, drive contract, 16k scaling

### The entire pre-fix collection is unusable

An independent check of `data/overnight/bc_150.npz` compared each physical
command with the pose delta it produced over roughly 998k transitions:

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---:|
| `corr(commanded omega, achieved omega)` | +0.021 |
| `corr(commanded v, achieved speed)` | -0.030 |
| mean commanded `|omega|` | 1.52 rad/s |
| mean achieved `|omega|` | 0.19 rad/s |

Median achieved yaw rate was effectively zero in every command bin, including
386k samples near -1.9 rad/s and 325k near +1.9 rad/s. Commands of 0.4--0.5 m/s
produced a median speed of 0.020 m/s, below the 0.068 m/s median while commanding
zero. One 899-step episode held `(v, omega) = (0.375, -1.31)` for 45 seconds but
moved only 7 cm and did not rotate.

The oracle labels were valid because the oracle reads the flow field. The state
distribution was not: it consisted of states visited by a base that drifted
independently of its command. All root-level `data/bc_*.npz` shards and all of
`data/overnight/` predate the fix and must never be used for training.
`data/overnight/bc_003.npz` is additionally a corrupt 170-byte partial ZIP.

### Holding the wheel-drop joints was necessary but not sufficient

The imported wheel-drop joints had previously been unactuated. They are now
held at their nominal position with high stiffness and damping, eliminating the
free suspension that unloaded wheels and created lateral slip.

Straight wheel commands then tracked, but opposing wheel speeds still scrubbed
the imported finite-width cylinder contacts instead of turning the base. That
geometry is not a tire model, and tuning actuator gains could not give it a
reliable differential-drive response.

The task action is a planar base Twist, so `Sim2EdgeNavEnv` now integrates
`(v, omega)` into an independent planar pose target at the 200 Hz physics rate
and writes the corresponding root pose and velocity. Wheel joints are still
animated from differential-drive inverse kinematics. Reset synchronizes the
planar target with the new episode pose so state cannot leak between episodes.

### Collection now refuses to start without a drive-response check

`collect_bc.py` runs a mandatory preflight before allocating the recorder or
writing a sample. It applies each of these commands for 50 ticks:

- turn in place: `(v, omega) = (0, 1.0)`;
- drive straight: `(v, omega) = (0.3, 0)`.

Achieved linear and angular response must be within 20% of the commanded axis,
with limits on cross-axis motion. `--check` uses the same assertion, so the
lightweight probe and real collection cannot diverge again. The real Isaac
check measured exactly 1.000 rad/s and 0.300 m/s.

Tests after the change: **116 passed, 2 skipped**.

### Small post-fix dataset proved the fix before scaling

`data/postfix_verify/bc_000.npz` contains 29,204 transitions from 128 episodes
(22.9 MB): 125 reached, 3 collided, and none timed out. Command response was:

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---:|
| `corr(omega)` | 0.99993 |
| `corr(v)` | 0.99962 |
| angular MAE | 0.0045 rad/s |
| linear MAE | 0.0032 m/s |

`scripts/verify_shard.py` passed every structure, label, perturbation, outcome,

### Scaled collection to 16,384 environments

The new run uses a clean output directory and 200k-transition shards:

```bash
~/isaac-venv/bin/python scripts/collect_bc.py \
  --num_envs 16384 \
  --episodes 500000 \
  --num_layouts 512 \
  --seed 24680 \
  --sigma 0.15 \
  --shard_steps 200000 \
  --out data/overnight_v2
```

At this scale PhysX requested a `foundLostPairsCapacity` of 6,668,288. The old
`2**22` allocation would miss interactions, so `NavEnvCfg.sim` now uses
`2**23 = 8,388,608`, about 26% headroom. Any future PhysX capacity warning still
invalidates the run; it is not safe to ignore merely because collection keeps
writing.

Live verification of completed shard `bc_059.npz` found 200,110 transitions
across 786 episodes: 759 reached, 27 collided, and none timed out. The original
failure-mode calculation on 199,324 consecutive transitions gave:

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---:|
| `corr(commanded omega, achieved omega)` | 0.999913 |
| `corr(commanded v, achieved speed)` | 0.999337 |
| angular MAE | 0.004820 rad/s |
| linear MAE | 0.004301 m/s |
| mean commanded / achieved `|omega|` | 0.495 / 0.495 rad/s |

Recent completed shards arrived about every five seconds, or roughly **39--40k
kept samples/s** after warm-up. At the measurement point the collector used
about 20.4 GiB of host RAM and 11.2 GiB of RTX 4090 memory. Neither the Isaac
log nor the Isaac Lab log contained a PhysX capacity, CUDA, or out-of-memory
error after the larger buffer was applied.

For scaling comparisons, `kept-samples/s` is the primary metric because it is
actual usable-data throughput. `env-steps/s` is only gross simulator throughput.
Compare after 1,200--2,000 steps (or several completed episode waves), and also
watch the `sim`, `rec`, and `write` timing split. The progress figures are
cumulative, so early readings are not representative.
