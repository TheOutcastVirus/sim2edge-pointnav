# 2026-08-01 — Closing the loop on the belief policy

The belief run from last night finished: 56,360 steps, 63 minutes, final belief
error 1.22 cm against a dead-reckoning baseline of 1.81 cm. A 33% improvement,
and every number in it measured over teacher-forced 32-step windows.

Today built the closed-loop evaluation those numbers were missing and ran it.
**The odometry head is net-harmful in closed loop** — it loses to its own
zeroed-weights baseline at every noise tier. The cause is located and it is not
the architecture: the training slip model and the simulator slip model disagree
in *sign structure*, and the head learned a correction that points a direction
the simulator's physics never produces.

Two other things fell out of the same run: the actor regressed 6.4 points during
joint training, and the corrected sensor model costs the old PPO policy 36
points.

---

## 1. What was missing

Everything reported about this design so far came from `belief_losses` over
32-step windows, where the belief is reset to truth at the start of each window
and the actor's own mistakes never re-enter its inputs. Both are the opposite of
deployment:

* episodes are **220 steps mean, 397 max** (measured on the validation shard),
  so the training window was a **7× extrapolation**;
* the actor drives on the belief, so an error in the belief changes the
  trajectory, which changes the next observation. Teacher forcing severs that.

`scripts/eval_belief.py` closes it. The belief is initialized once from the true
relative goal and then integrates its own estimate for the whole episode. Four
modes run against identical layouts, and the *differences* are the measurement:

| mode | actor | goal source | isolates |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reference` | `runs/ppo_v1/best.pt` | odometry pose | the pre-belief number |
| `pose` | belief run | odometry pose | what joint training did to the actor |
| `dead-reckoning` | belief run | belief, head zeroed | option A, the baseline |
| `belief` | belief run | belief | the deliverable |

`dead-reckoning` zeroes `odometry.head` rather than reimplementing option A.
The residual parameterization makes the baseline the zero-weights special case of
the model, so this needs no second code path that could drift from the one being
measured.

## 2. Results

512 episodes, 512 layouts, layout seed 10,000,000 — the same seed and count as
`runs/ppo_v1/eval.json`, so these compare directly. Written to
`runs/belief_v1/eval.json` (untracked; `runs/` is gitignored).

| mode | SR 0× | SR 1× | SR 2× | belief err @ end (0×/1×/2×) | drift/m 0× |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `reference` | 58.2% | 59.6% | 59.8% | — | — |
| `pose` | 51.8% | 52.1% | 49.6% | — | — |
| `dead-reckoning` | 50.4% | 49.0% | 47.5% | 4.4 / 6.2 / 8.5 cm | 2.11 cm/m |
| `belief` | 48.4% | 47.5% | 46.9% | 7.2 / 8.3 / 10.3 cm | 3.09 cm/m |

For reference, `runs/ppo_v1/eval.json` on these same layouts: **94.5% / 94.9% /
91.0%**, collision 5.3%.

`belief_error_max` sits within 0–7% of `belief_error_final` at every tier — equal
at 0× (4.4/4.4 and 7.2/7.2), diverging slightly as noise rises (8.5 vs 9.1 at 2×
dead reckoning). The error therefore grows close to monotonically and essentially
never recovers, consistent with the bias-dominated linear growth seen in the
training curve and with the no-loop-closure limit recorded in yesterday's §9.

## 3. The odometry head is net-harmful in closed loop

It loses to `dead-reckoning` on every belief metric at every tier: 7.2 vs 4.4 cm
final error at 0×, 3.09 vs 2.11 cm/m drift, and about 2 points of success rate.
Confirmed first at 64 episodes, then at 512 across three noise tiers, so this is
not sampling noise.

**This is the exact reverse of the training metric**, which had the head beating
dead reckoning by 33%. A 32-step teacher-forced window could not have shown it.

The head is not broken on the distribution it was trained on. Measured on held-out
validation windows, against the residual it is asked to predict:

| | mean | std |
|---|---:|---:|
| target residual (forward) | −0.015 cm | 0.073 cm |
| head correction (forward) | −0.014 cm | 0.050 cm |

R² 0.407, correlation 0.640, per-tick error 0.056 → 0.041 cm. It works. It just
works on the wrong distribution.

---

## 4. Where the transfer fails: two slip models that disagree in sign

`dataset._slip` (training, load-time stand-in) and `nav_task._slip_factor`
(simulator) were built to have "the same multiplicative structure." They do not.
Sampling both over a plausible ω range, expressing the residual as a fraction of
the commanded tick:

| | mean | std | **P(residual > 0)** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| training (`dataset._slip`) | −1.15% | 3.75% | **39.2%** |
| simulator (`nav_task._slip_factor`) | −2.01% | 2.46% | **0.0%** |

The mechanism is one clamp each, pointing opposite ways:

* `nav_task.py:687` — `(1.0 - slip).clamp(0.5, 1.0)`. The upper bound at 1.0
  means the robot can never travel further than commanded. One-sided.
* `dataset.py:334` — `slip.clamp(-0.5, 0.5)`, then `1/(1-slip)`. Both signs
  survive. Two-sided.

So the head trained on a residual that is positive 39% of the time and met one
that is positive 0% of the time. Roughly four ticks in ten it pushes the belief
*forward*, a direction the simulator's physics never produces. The magnitudes are
off too — 1.75× in the mean, 1.5× in the spread — but the sign structure is the
part that makes the correction actively wrong rather than merely undersized.

This also predicts the arrival behaviour we measured, which is the sort of
confirmation worth writing down: `belief` announces arrival **more** often than
`dead-reckoning` (50.8% vs 36.1%) while being **less** accurate when it does
(32.7 vs 31.0 cm true error). A head that sometimes shoves the belief goalward is
exactly what produces that pair.

## 5. What the head actually learned

Zero-ablating each input group and measuring the change in the forward
correction, on held-out windows:

| input | mean \|Δ\| |
|---|---:|
| `prev_action` | **0.0374 cm** |
| `lidar` | 0.0102 cm |
| `gyro` | 0.0082 cm |

The command dominates the gyro by 4.6× and the lidar by 3.7×. **The head is
mostly a learned re-scaling of dead reckoning, not a lidar-based ego-motion
estimator.** That is why it transfers badly: a function of the command inherits
the command-conditional of whichever slip model produced its labels, and carries
no independent evidence to fall back on.

It also undercuts the premise in `models/belief.py` — "lidar is poor at rotation
and good at translation, and the gyro is the other way round." The lidar's
contribution here is on par with the gyro's, and both are minor. The head's own
docstring nominates raising its angular resolution as the first knob to reach
for; that now has evidence behind it, but only after the slip mismatch is fixed,
because the current learning signal does not reward looking at the scan.

## 6. The actor regressed 6.4 points

`reference` 58.2% → `pose` 51.8%. Identical layouts, identical goal source; the
actor is the only thing swapped.

The run was joint, not head-only: `freeze_actor=False`, `actor_lr_scale=0.1`.
All 18 actor tensors moved, up to **65% relative** on `trunk.0.weight`. Behaviour
moved with them — on 24,576 held-out observations, ω differs from the PPO actor
by 0.215 rad/s mean and 0.504 p95, against a PPO ω spread of 0.759 std.

The direction is the diagnosis: **MSE to the corpus action fell 0.0404 → 0.0041**,
a 10× move *toward* the DAgger targets. With `action_coef=1.0` against corpus
labels for 56k steps, the actor was pulled from its PPO optimum back toward
imitation. BC on this corpus was 49%; PPO reached 94.5%. The 8× action-loss drop
during training, which read as "adapting to swept scans," was substantially
regression to the BC objective.

## 7. The corrected sensor model costs the old policy 36 points

`reference` is `runs/ppo_v1/best.pt`, the 94.5% policy, on its own evaluation
layouts: **94.5% → 58.2%**, collisions 5.3% → 41.8%.

At 0× noise the goal path in this evaluator is bit-identical to `eval_bc.py`'s
(both reduce to the exact pose), so the entire gap is the spinning lidar, deskew,
and wheel slip — the three things §7 and §5.5 of yesterday's log added. This is
what `allow_legacy_sensor_model` exists to measure, and it is the largest single
number produced today.

It reframes the success rates above: at ~40–50% collisions across all modes,
**success is now limited by collision avoidance, not localization**. Belief errors
of 4–10 cm sit far inside the 25 cm tolerance and barely move SR. That is why
`pose`, `dead-reckoning` and `belief` land within 3 points of each other despite
meaningfully different belief quality.

The 1× and 2× tiers here are *not* comparable to `runs/ppo_v1/eval.json`: this
evaluator applies the random-walk `OdometryDrift` to pose modes, which
`eval_bc.py` never did.

## 8. Arrival detection is the real failure, and `success_rate` cannot see it

At every belief tier, when the robot concludes it has arrived it is truly
**31–34 cm** away — outside the 0.25 m tolerance. Only 36–51% of episodes ever
reach that conclusion at all.

`success_rate` is blind to this because the environment terminates on the *true*
pose. On hardware nothing does that; the belief is the only arrival signal there
is. This is the §3 problem — "the policy must know it is within 0.25 m of a thing
it cannot see" — and it is unsolved.

The sign is consistent with slip in both modes: the robot believes it moved the
commanded distance, moves less, and concludes it has arrived early.

Both criteria are now reported per tier, `success_rate` and `belief_arrival_*`.
Any future number quoted for this design should be the second one.

## 9. Open decision: which slip model is right

They cannot both stay. The simulator's systematic term is
`randn * WHEEL_SLIP_SCALE` — zero-mean, documented as wheel radius, tyre wear and
floor — but the clamp at 1.0 rectifies it, so roughly half of all episodes have
their systematic component silently truncated toward zero. That is not a
per-episode systematic slip; it is a half-rectified draw, and it is a defect
independent of the training mismatch.

The distinction that decides it: **calibration error is two-sided, slip is not.**
An oversized wheel radius makes the robot travel *further* than commanded, and
that is a real odometry error mode. Scrub and wheelspin only ever lose ground.
That argues for splitting the terms — let the systematic/calibration term exceed
1.0 in the simulator, keep the random and turn terms loss-only — and making
`dataset._slip` match whatever is chosen.

Not changed today. It should be settled **before** recollection, because
recollection bakes it in.

## 10. Known limits of this evaluation

* **The stand-in slip is still a stand-in.** Fixing the sign mismatch would make
  the two models agree; it would not make either of them a real robot. Only
  recollection against the corrected simulator removes the load-time
  augmentation.
* **One layout generator.** These are held-out layouts from the same generator,
  so this remains in-distribution generalization. The adversarial axes from
  yesterday's §8 are still unbuilt, and the 40–50% collision rate says they would
  now find plenty.
* **Spin rate unconfirmed.** `LIDAR_SPIN_HZ = 5.5` is still not checked against
  the A1's actual configuration, and §7's 36-point result is a direct function of
  it.
* **Deskew reads a one-tick-stale command.** The offline loader deskews with the
  command *at* the frame; online that value has not been decided yet, so the
  evaluator uses the command just executed. The resulting offset is far below the
  range noise being corrected, but the two paths are not bit-identical.

## 11. What was built

* `scripts/eval_belief.py` — the four-mode closed-loop evaluator.
* `tests/test_eval_belief.py` — 14 tests against a kinematic stand-in
  environment, CPU, ~2 s, no Isaac. Covers the sweep ring buffer, the gyro
  history window, the no-integration-at-t=0 rule, non-destructive head zeroing,
  and pose-independence of the belief path.

Suite: **235 passed, 2 skipped** (221 pre-existing, 14 new).

Two of the new tests were mutation-checked rather than trusted: forcing
integration at `t=0` trips `test_first_tick_does_not_integrate`, and silently
resyncing the belief to truth each tick trips
`test_dead_reckoning_drift_tracks_the_slip_it_cannot_see`. A test asserting the
belief never reads the pose passes under both mutations and is therefore weaker
than it looks — translation invariance does not distinguish "integrates" from
"peeks at a relative truth."

## 12. Status and order of work

The retrain proposed yesterday — `--freeze-actor`, longer `seq_len`, stop near
the ~27k plateau — would fix §6 but not §3, and §3 is the part that is actively
wrong. Order:

1. **Settle §9**, then make `dataset._slip` and `nav_task._slip_factor` agree.
2. **Recollect** against the corrected simulator. Until then the head's learning
   signal is a stand-in it is demonstrably overfitting to, and tuning it is
   premature.
3. **Retrain the actor against swept scans regardless.** §7 applies to the whole
   project, not just the belief work: the 94.5% policy is a 58.2% policy against
   an honestly modelled sensor, and collisions — not localization — are now the
   binding constraint.
4. Only then revisit the head, starting with its angular resolution (§5).

One piece of hygiene: `best.pt` from the belief run is selected on a *training*
batch (`train_belief.py:141`), not validation. The 5 validation shards are loaded
and counted (`:78`) but never scored, and the field is nonetheless named
`best_validation`. The reported 0.74 cm is the luckiest of 2,819 logged batches
against a band mean of ~1.19 cm. **Use `final.pt`**, and either wire validation in
or rename the field.
