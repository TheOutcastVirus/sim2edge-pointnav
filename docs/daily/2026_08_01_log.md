# 2026-08-01 — Closing the loop on the belief policy

The belief run from last night finished: 56,360 steps, 63 minutes, final
belief error 1.22 cm against a dead-reckoning baseline of 1.81 cm — a 33%
improvement, measured over teacher-forced 32-step windows.

Today built the closed-loop evaluation those numbers were missing.
**The odometry head is net-harmful in closed loop** — it loses to its own
zeroed-weights baseline at every noise tier. The cause: the training slip
model and the simulator slip model disagree in *sign structure*, so the head
learned a correction pointing a direction the simulator's physics never
produces. Also found in the same run: the actor regressed 6.4 points during
joint training, and the corrected sensor model costs the old PPO policy 36
points.

---

## 1. What was missing

Everything reported so far came from `belief_losses` over 32-step windows,
where the belief resets to truth at each window start and the actor's own
mistakes never re-enter its inputs — the opposite of deployment: episodes
run **220 steps mean, 397 max** (a 7× extrapolation past the training
window), and the actor drives on the belief, so a belief error changes the
trajectory, which changes the next observation. Teacher forcing severs that.

`scripts/eval_belief.py` closes it: the belief initializes once from the
true relative goal, then integrates its own estimate for the whole episode.
Four modes run against identical layouts:

| mode | actor | goal source | isolates |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reference` | `runs/ppo_v1/best.pt` | odometry pose | the pre-belief number |
| `pose` | belief run | odometry pose | what joint training did to the actor |
| `dead-reckoning` | belief run | belief, head zeroed | option A, the baseline |
| `belief` | belief run | belief | the deliverable |

`dead-reckoning` zeroes `odometry.head` rather than reimplementing option A
— the residual parameterization makes the baseline the zero-weights special
case, so no second code path can drift from the one being measured.

## 3. The odometry head is net-harmful in closed loop

Loses to `dead-reckoning` on every belief metric at every tier (7.2 vs 4.4 cm
final error at 0×, 3.09 vs 2.11 cm/m drift, ~2 points of success), confirmed
at 64 then 512 episodes across three tiers. This is the exact reverse of the
training metric (head beat dead reckoning by 33%) — a 32-step teacher-forced
window couldn't have shown it.

The head isn't broken on its own training distribution:

| | mean | std |
|---|---:|---:|
| target residual (forward) | −0.015 cm | 0.073 cm |
| head correction (forward) | −0.014 cm | 0.050 cm |

R² 0.407, correlation 0.640, per-tick error 0.056→0.041 cm. It works — just
on the wrong distribution.

## 4. Where the transfer fails: two slip models that disagree in sign

`dataset._slip` (training) and `nav_task._slip_factor` (simulator) were
meant to share "the same multiplicative structure." They don't. Sampling
both over a plausible ω range, residual as a fraction of the commanded tick:

| | mean | std | **P(residual > 0)** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| training (`dataset._slip`) | −1.15% | 3.75% | **39.2%** |
| simulator (`nav_task._slip_factor`) | −2.01% | 2.46% | **0.0%** |

One clamp each, pointing opposite ways: `nav_task.py:687` —
`(1.0 - slip).clamp(0.5, 1.0)`, upper-bounded at 1.0 so the robot can never
travel further than commanded (one-sided); `dataset.py:334` —
`slip.clamp(-0.5, 0.5)` then `1/(1-slip)`, both signs survive (two-sided).
The head trained on a residual positive 39% of the time and met one positive
0% of the time — roughly 4 ticks in 10 it pushes the belief *forward*, a
direction the simulator's physics never produces. Magnitudes are off too
(1.75× mean, 1.5× spread), but the sign flip is what makes the correction
actively wrong rather than merely undersized.

This predicts the arrival behaviour measured: `belief` announces arrival
**more** often than `dead-reckoning` (50.8% vs 36.1%) while being **less**
accurate when it does (32.7 vs 31.0 cm true error) — exactly what a head
that sometimes shoves the belief goalward produces.

## 5. What the head actually learned

Zero-ablating each input group, change in forward correction on held-out
windows:

| input | mean \|Δ\| |
|---|---:|
| `prev_action` | **0.0374 cm** |
| `lidar` | 0.0102 cm |
| `gyro` | 0.0082 cm |

The command dominates gyro by 4.6× and lidar by 3.7×. **The head is mostly a
learned re-scaling of dead reckoning, not a lidar-based ego-motion
estimator** — a function of the command inherits the command-conditional of
whichever slip model produced its labels, with no independent evidence to
fall back on. This undercuts `models/belief.py`'s premise ("lidar is poor at
rotation and good at translation, gyro the reverse") — lidar's contribution
here is on par with gyro's, and both are minor. Raising the head's angular
resolution is the first knob to reach for, but only after the slip mismatch
is fixed — the current learning signal doesn't reward looking at the scan.

## 6. The actor regressed 6.4 points

`reference` 58.2% → `pose` 51.8%, identical layouts and goal source — only
the actor changed. The run was joint (`freeze_actor=False`,
`actor_lr_scale=0.1`); all 18 actor tensors moved, up to **65% relative** on
`trunk.0.weight`. Behaviour moved with them: ω differs from the PPO actor by
0.215 rad/s mean / 0.504 p95 on 24,576 held-out observations, against a PPO
ω spread of 0.759 std.

MSE to the corpus action fell 0.0404→0.0041, a 10× move *toward* DAgger
targets. With `action_coef=1.0` against corpus labels for 56k steps, the
actor was pulled from its PPO optimum back toward imitation (BC on this
corpus was 49%; PPO reached 94.5%). The 8× action-loss drop during
training, read as "adapting to swept scans," was substantially regression to
the BC objective.

## 7. The corrected sensor model costs the old policy 36 points

`reference` is `runs/ppo_v1/best.pt` on its own evaluation layouts: **94.5%
→ 58.2%**, collisions 5.3% → 41.8%. At 0× noise the goal path here is
bit-identical to `eval_bc.py`'s, so the entire gap is the spinning lidar,
deskew, and wheel slip added yesterday (§5.5, §7) — the largest single
number produced today.

This reframes the success rates above: at ~40–50% collisions across all
modes, **success is now limited by collision avoidance, not localization**.
Belief errors of 4–10 cm sit far inside the 25 cm tolerance and barely move
SR — why `pose`, `dead-reckoning` and `belief` land within 3 points of each
other despite meaningfully different belief quality. (1× and 2× tiers here
aren't comparable to `runs/ppo_v1/eval.json`, which never applied the
random-walk `OdometryDrift` to pose modes.)

## 8. Arrival detection is the real failure, and `success_rate` can't see it

At every belief tier, when the robot concludes it has arrived it is truly
**31–34 cm** away — outside the 0.25 m tolerance — and only 36–51% of
episodes ever reach that conclusion. `success_rate` is blind to this because
the environment terminates on the *true* pose; on hardware nothing does
that, the belief is the only arrival signal. This is the unsolved "the
policy must know it is within 0.25 m of a thing it cannot see" problem
(§3). The sign is consistent with slip: the robot believes it moved the
commanded distance, moves less, and concludes it arrived early.

Both criteria are now reported per tier, `success_rate` and
`belief_arrival_*`. Any future number should use the second.

---

## 9. Open decision: which slip model is right

They can't both stay. The simulator's systematic term is
`randn * WHEEL_SLIP_SCALE` — zero-mean, meant to represent wheel radius,
tyre wear, floor — but the clamp at 1.0 rectifies it, silently truncating
roughly half of all episodes' systematic component toward zero: a
half-rectified draw, not a per-episode systematic slip, and a defect
independent of the training mismatch.

**Calibration error is two-sided, slip is not**: an oversized wheel radius
makes the robot travel *further* than commanded (real), while scrub and
wheelspin only ever lose ground. Argues for splitting the terms — let the
systematic/calibration term exceed 1.0 in the simulator, keep random/turn
terms loss-only — and matching `dataset._slip` to whatever is chosen. Not
changed today; should be settled **before** recollection, which bakes it in.

## 10. Known limits of this evaluation

* **The stand-in slip is still a stand-in** — fixing the sign mismatch makes
  the two models agree, not either of them a real robot.
* **One layout generator** — still in-distribution generalization; the
  adversarial axes from yesterday (§8) remain unbuilt, and 40–50% collision
  says they'd find plenty.
* **Spin rate unconfirmed** — `LIDAR_SPIN_HZ = 5.5` still not checked
  against the A1, and §7's 36-point result is a direct function of it.
* **Deskew reads a one-tick-stale command** — offline loader deskews with
  the command *at* the frame; online uses the command just executed. Effect
  is far below the range noise being corrected, but paths aren't bit-identical.

### Current handoff / cost decision

Do **not** resume v18 or run more epochs of v15/v16/v18. Three increasingly
expressive estimator adaptations have plateaued on the same corpus. The
pipeline is substantially complete and the pose-fed actor passes its
screen, but the end-to-end sensor-only system is not accepted: best full
closed-loop nominal result remains 75.8% navigation success, 72.7% correct
declared arrival, 7.8% false arrival, 10.2% collision, 11.5 cm mean, 29.0 cm
p95, 2.94 cm/m drift, against gates of 85%/5%/10%/5 cm/12.5 cm/2 cm/m.

This is evidence of a localization observability/modeling ceiling in the
current simulator/sensor formulation, not evidence that another long
training run would finish the project. No additional user-run training is
required at this stopping point. Any continuation should begin with a
bounded design/observability experiment (e.g. scan-matching or a local-map
memory baseline using only lidar/IMU) and must demonstrate a large offline
localization gain before collecting data or launching PPO again.
