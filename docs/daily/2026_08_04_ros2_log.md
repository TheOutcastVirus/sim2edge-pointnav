# 2026-08-04 (second) — The stack is C++ and on the board, and the scheduler was the constraint

The morning log ended with "the C++ node and preprocessing parity" as the next
piece of work. It exists now: `ros2_ws/` is a six-package ROS 2 workspace that
builds on the training host and on the Ventuno from the same source tree, and
the whole control tick has been replayed through it on the board against the
INT8 actor on the Hexagon NPU.

## Outcome

| | Python, this board | C++, this board |
|---|---:|---:|
| localizer, production 128/2 | 21–27 ms | **6.9 ms** |
| whole tick (deskew + bin + localize + actor) | — | **6.9 ms** standalone |
| whole tick, under the live ROS graph | — | **9.8 ms** median, 20.1 p95, 26.4 max |
| share of the 50 ms period | 42–53% (localizer alone) | **20% median, 53% worst** |

Zero ticks missed the period across 2,114 ticks of the live graph. Preprocessing
agrees with the Python reference to float32 epsilon. And the thing that decided
whether any of this closed was not the algorithm.

## The finding: this is a big.LITTLE board and the localizer must not run on a LITTLE core

The Ventuno's QCS8275 is 4× Cortex-A78C (`cpu0-3`, 2.1–2.36 GHz) plus 4×
Cortex-A55 (`cpu4-7`, 1.96 GHz). The same binary, the same replay, the same
configuration, differing only in `taskset`:

| pinned to | localizer median | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `cpu2` (A78C) | **6.98 ms** | 16.3 ms | 21.8 ms |
| `cpu6` (A55) | **53.57 ms** | 140.5 ms | 184.4 ms |

**7.7×, against a clock ratio of 1.2×.** That gap is microarchitecture, not
frequency: scan-to-submap matching is memory-bound nearest-neighbour work over
a 40,000-point cloud, and an in-order A55 with small caches is close to the
worst case for it. On an A55 the localizer alone is 107% of the control period.

Left to the scheduler, the navigator migrates, and the loop misses its deadline
whenever it does. Measured over the live graph:

| | median | p95 | max | ticks over 50 ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| unpinned | 14.4 ms | 32.3 ms | 64.4 ms | **35 of ~2,200** |
| pinned to `cpu2,3` | **9.8 ms** | **20.1 ms** | **26.4 ms** | **0 of 2,114** |

`nav_cpus` therefore defaults to `2,3` in both launch files and is documented
there as a correctness setting rather than tuning. This is the single most
important thing on this page: a correct stack that lands on the wrong core
silently becomes a robot that acts on 65 ms-old decisions.

The first measurement of the live graph was taken with `ros2 topic echo`
subscribing to `nav/status`, which is a Python process at 20 Hz on the same
board. Removing it changed the median by 0.1 ms — so it was not the cause, but
it was a confound worth eliminating, and the percentiles now live inside the
navigator where measuring them is free.

## What agrees with Python, and what deliberately does not

`sim2edge_parity` replays 4,514 recorded ticks from 12 held-out episodes through
the production code path and compares against what Python produced from the same
inputs. The gate is asymmetric on purpose.

**Preprocessing is gated and passes at float32 epsilon.**

| | mean | max |
|---|---:|---:|
| deskewed range | 9.4e-7 m | 2.9e-6 m |
| deskewed bearing | 3.0e-7 rad | 4.8e-7 rad |
| bin-edge crossings | — | **2 of 3,250,080 beams** |

Two beams out of three and a quarter million landed in a different bin than
Python chose. That is not a defect and it cannot be removed: binning is a
`floor()`, so two implementations agreeing on a bearing to 5e-7 rad still
disagree when that bearing sits within 5e-7 of an edge. Gating on the maximum
bin difference would fail the transcription for arithmetic that is correct, so
the gate is on the *geometry* plus the crossing *rate*, and the bin figures are
reported.

**The localizer is measured, not gated.** It was ported speed-first, and the
divergence is the price:

| | mean | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| pose translation | 0.028 cm | 0.134 cm | 0.224 cm |
| pose yaw | 0.0012° | 0.0047° | 0.0091° |

0.28 mm of mean divergence against a per-tick slip signal of 0.078 cm is
comfortable, and the **observability flag agrees on all 4,514 ticks** — the two
implementations never disagree about whether a scan was usable, which is the
decision that actually changes behaviour.

**An unplanned cross-check fell out of this.** Comparing the board's INT8 QNN
actor against the FP32 checkpoint through the C++ path gives 0.42% mean and
1.71% max of the omega limit. The morning's lowering report, computed entirely
in Python on a different code path, says 0.42% and 1.73%. The quantization
error reproduced independently.

That comparison was nearly reported wrong. The first replay recorded the
corpus's stored *oracle* command as its reference, so the "actor command" row
was measuring how far the policy is from its teacher — a policy-quality number
under a label that reads like a quantization number. The replay format now
carries a flag saying which reference it holds and the report names the row
accordingly.

## What the speed came from

Not vectorization, not threads. The port is single-threaded and the arithmetic
is the same arithmetic.

1. **A uniform bucket grid replaces `scipy.cKDTree`.** The tree was rebuilt over
   ~40,000 points every tick — O(N log N) with a large constant, paid in full
   though the geometry changed by one scan. The grid builds by counting sort in
   O(N). It is *exact*, not approximate: ICP already rejects correspondences
   beyond 0.20 m, so sizing the cell at the rejection radius makes a 3×3 cell
   scan an exact search over precisely the region that can produce a usable
   match. `knn` expands rings until the covered distance exceeds the k-th best,
   and both are tested against brute force on clustered, collinear and sparse
   layouts.
2. **Surface normals are memoized per submap point for the duration of a tick.**
   Python recomputes a 7-NN PCA normal on every ICP iteration; successive
   iterations re-match largely the same points, so this is pure memoization —
   identical values, roughly a fifth of the queries.
3. **Point clouds are float, the pose is double.** The clouds are measurements
   with centimetre noise and halving their memory traffic is most of the rest.
   The pose integrates over hundreds of ticks and float there would accumulate
   rounding into the drift term this tracker exists to measure.

The full sweep, on the board, pinned:

| submap | stride | median | p95 | max | submap points |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **128** | **2** | **6.90 ms** | 13.8 ms | 21.7 ms | 40,407 |
| 128 | 4 | 2.08 ms | 3.9 ms | 6.0 ms | 20,233 |
| 64 | 2 | 4.52 ms | 8.7 ms | 15.5 ms | 21,922 |
| 64 | 4 | 1.41 ms | 2.5 ms | 4.3 ms | 10,977 |
| 32 | 2 | 2.67 ms | 5.0 ms | 8.4 ms | 11,118 |
| 32 | 4 | 0.89 ms | 1.5 ms | 2.4 ms | 5,567 |

`point_stride 4` still halves the dominant cost and **still has not been
evaluated for accuracy**. The morning log said so; it remains true, and the
extra headroom is a reason not to need it rather than a reason to take it.

## Three things the bring-up cost, none of them algorithmic

**The replay's cold start was mistaken for a deskew bug.** The first parity run
showed deskewed bearings differing by up to 0.064 rad — 3.7°, far outside
float32 noise. The cause was that the corpus offers gyro and speed from *before*
an episode's first servable tick, which a robot powering up does not have. The
C++ correctly starts with an empty deskew history; Python was handed one. The
exporter now records the cold start the hardware actually performs, and the
divergence fell to 4.8e-7 rad. A reference no correct implementation can reach
is worse than no reference.

**`libqnn_executorch_backend.so` is a shared library.** Every node died with
exit code 127, which `ros2 launch` reports as "process has died" and nothing
else. `CMAKE_INSTALL_RPATH_USE_LINK_PATH` fixes it properly; requiring
`LD_LIBRARY_PATH` to be right would make the stack depend on a variable that is
easy to lose and silent when wrong.

**A service and a topic have no ordering guarantee between them.** The replay
node reset the navigator and published the episode's goal in the same callback;
the reset landed second and cleared the goal it had just been given. The symptom
was a believed goal distance pinned at 0.00 m for a whole episode, which reads
exactly like a localization failure. The goal is now published from the reset's
response callback, and the replay waits for the navigator to be discoverable
before it starts.

## What is in `ros2_ws/`

```
sim2edge_core/     no ROS: the spec header generated from spec.py, deskew,
                   binning, the localizer, the ExecuTorch actor runner, and the
                   Navigator that owns one control tick. Plus sim2edge_parity
                   and sim2edge_bench, which run on a laptop.
sim2edge_msgs/     LocalizerState, NavStatus
sim2edge_nodes/    navigator_node (the whole tick), safety_node (owns /cmd_vel),
                   replay_node (publishes a corpus as real sensor topics)
oak_imu/           the OAK-D's gyro, depthai v3, at the sensor's own rate
sllidar_ros2/      vendored upstream A1 driver, pinned, unmodified
sim2edge_bringup/  launch files, the Create 3 usb0 DDS profile, ventuno_env.sh
```

The navigator is **one** node and not four. Splitting deskew, localization and
inference apart would push a 720-point sweep and a 731-float observation through
DDS twice per tick and buy nothing — no stage is independently useful and none
can run while another waits. The safety layer *is* a separate process, and
deliberately: it owns `/cmd_vel` so that it keeps stopping the robot when the
navigator does not.

`Navigator::tick` is the only implementation of the tick. The node calls it, the
parity harness calls it, the benchmark calls it. A harness with its own copy of
the ordering would prove things about the harness.

## What is not done

1. **No robot trial, and no hardware attached.** The board has no lidar on
   `/dev/ttyUSB*` and no OAK-D connected. `sllidar_ros2` and `oak_imu` build and
   are launched by `robot.launch.py`, and neither has ever seen its device.
   `LIDAR_SPIN_HZ = 5.5` and the A1's real beam count remain nominal figures.
2. **The quantization gate still has not been run.** The action error
   reproduced at 0.42%/1.71%, but the inherited acceptance bar is a **sim
   success-rate drop under 2 points**, not an action error, precisely because a
   small action error can cost success at the moments that matter. That needs
   `eval_localized.py` on the host driving the INT8 graph. It is still the most
   important next step and nothing should be built on this `.pte` before it.
3. **The replay is open-loop.** The corpus was oracle-driven, so the recorded
   pose does not respond to what the policy commands. Everything here is an
   input/output and latency result on the target silicon; none of it is a
   sim-to-real result.
4. **The Create 3 path is untested.** `/odom` and `/cmd_vel` are wired at the
   root namespace as this base's Iron firmware expects, and the usb0 DDS profile
   is written down, but no base has been connected.

## Order of work

1. The quantization gate on the host. It has been the top of this list since
   this morning and the C++ existing does not change that.
2. Attach the A1 and confirm its actual spin rate and beam count against
   `spec.LIDAR_SPIN_HZ`. Deskew reads `time_increment` from the message and
   falls back to the nominal rate, so a mismatch degrades quietly rather than
   failing — which is exactly why it must be checked rather than assumed.
3. Attach the OAK-D and confirm the gyro's sign and frame against
   `angular_velocity.z`. A sign error here inverts deskew and would look like a
   localization failure.
4. Then, and only then, a stationary robot trial: drift with the wheels off the
   ground, before anything drives.
