# 2026-08-03 — Four fixes that worked, none of which were the constraint

A day of chasing the estimator, then the loss, then the sensor — finding the
ceiling one layer below wherever I was looking.

**The estimator was fixable and was not the constraint.** Least squares is
the wrong family for scans where a third of bearings are gross outliers: a
consensus vote recovers per-tick ego-motion at 0.96 cm/m drift where an
ICP-equivalent solve, on the *identical residuals in the same loop*, gets
89 cm/m — reproducing the 2026-08-02 ICP null result and locating it in the
estimator rather than the data. §2.

**The loss was fixable and was not the constraint either.** Per-tick
translation error was weighted 69× above the accumulated belief error it
stands in for, and per tick the command beats any scan estimate on variance
— gradient descent had been correctly ignoring the lidar for four runs.
Reweighting toward the integral more than doubled lidar causality again, to
+1.80 cm of shuffled degradation against 0.67 for the best checkpoint
without the vote. For the first time the estimator is genuinely
lidar-driven rather than a command rescaling. The held-out mean did not
improve. §2b, §2c.

**The ceiling is the sensor reconstruction, and the fix for it failed.**
Place one reconstructed frame into another using the *true* relative pose,
at zero sensor noise, and they still disagree by 1.1 cm one tick apart —
**14× the 0.078 cm/tick slip signal** any estimator here must measure.
Sub-tick beam placement, which the mechanism pointed at, changes nothing.
§6, §7.

**One thing that did land end-to-end.** The arrival bound was not
describing the error: the 1.96σ gate covered 9.5% of steps where a
calibrated bound covers 95%. That's a model-form error — a systematic scale
error propagated as a random walk — and it's fixed. §3.

Also: Phase 5 exists in part. The deployable step exports, reproduces eager
numerics exactly, and costs 0.69 ms of a 50 ms control period. §4.

**No end-to-end number moved and no closed-loop evaluation was run.** Best
closed-loop result is still 75.8% navigation success / 72.7% correct
declared arrival / 11.5 cm mean error, from 2026-08-01. Three of the four
things fixed today were correctly diagnosed, correctly implemented, and
worth nothing to the number — which is itself the finding, and §7 says
what's left.

## 2. The scan vote

### The argument

Yesterday established that motion is in the scans (median frame difference
tracks travel at ratio ~1.0) and that roughly a third of bearings are gross
outliers at depth edges. Together these are a precise indictment of *least
squares*, not the data.

A squared residual is unbounded — one outlier at a depth edge contributes
more to the objective than a hundred good bearings, and 30% of them don't
add noise, they relocate the minimum. Least squares has a breakdown point of
zero. Both solvers tried yesterday (point-to-line, full ICP) minimize
exactly that, which is why both returned nothing despite being exact on
analytic rooms.

Scoring by *agreement* inverts the failure. For each candidate translation
every bearing contributes

```
exp(-(dr + dx cos theta + dy sin theta)^2 / 2 sigma^2)
```

bounded in `[0, 1]`. An outlier's residual is large under every candidate,
so it contributes ~0 to all of them and cannot move the peak — it abstains
rather than votes.

### The measurement

`scripts/probe_scan_vote.py`, on `belief_onpolicy_v15` validation episodes
at 1× sensor noise, 976 per-tick samples, scored against true displacement:

| estimator | drift | scatter | mean error | corr(x) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| command dead reckoning | 2.10 cm/m | 0.10 cm | 0.08 cm | — |
| **consensus vote** | **0.96 cm/m** | 0.57 cm | 0.44 cm | 0.924 |
| least squares, same residuals | 89.3 cm/m | 11.0 cm | 7.65 cm | 0.084 |
| estimating zero | 100.0 cm/m | 0.73 cm | 1.90 cm | 0.0 |

The least-squares row is the control and the important one: computed from
the same `difference` and `weight` tensors the vote succeeds on, in the same
loop. It reproduces yesterday's ICP result — the data was never the
constraint.

**Read drift, not mean error — but read the scatter too.** Belief error is
an integral of these residuals, so a biased estimator accumulates linearly
while a zero-mean one accumulates as a random walk. The vote's mean error
(0.44 cm) is *worse* than the command's (0.08 cm) while its drift is less
than half — reads as a clear win. It is not, and §2b works out why: at 220
steps the two effects cancel almost exactly. Quoting drift alone would have
been the same mistake as quoting the mean alone, one column over.

### Two things that were easy to get wrong

**Refine the peak locally.** A soft-argmax over the whole candidate grid is
dragged toward the grid centre by whatever pedestal the surface carries.
The grid is centred on zero while the robot is really moving ~2 cm forward,
so that shrinkage appears as a systematic underestimate of travel — exactly
the component that integrates. Windowing the refinement around the hard
peak cut drift from 1.30 to 0.96 cm/m.

**Do not lengthen the baseline.** Looked like free signal — displacement
grows linearly with frame gap while range noise doesn't, so SNR should
improve. Measured:

| gap | vote drift |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 0.96 cm/m |
| 2 | 10.8 cm/m |
| 4 | 81.2 cm/m |
| 8 | 87.3 cm/m |

The range-flow model behind the vote is first-order in displacement and
compares equal bearings — both assumptions fail once the robot has moved
far enough to shift scene content between bearings and open/close
occlusions. Gap 1 isn't a tuning choice, it's the only regime where the
model holds. Recorded as a measured negative so it isn't retried.

### What is in the model

`ScanVote` holds **no parameters** and runs under `no_grad` — the
projection geometry is exact and the kernel width is the range noise, same
status as `sensors.deskew_history`: fixed sensor processing that happens to
sit inside the model. The learned part is how the head *reads* the surface:

* the vote's implied correction to the command, in units of the correction
  limit, so a near-identity weight is the solution;
* the surface itself, through a small `Conv2d` stack — an isolated peak, a
  ridge, and a broad plateau are three different reliabilities sharing one
  argmax;
* peak sharpness, fed to the confidence gate without passing through a
  proposal initialized near zero, which is the starvation §1 diagnosed —
  addressed structurally rather than through the loss.

Sharpness is honest about what it measures: it detects how much
bearing-diverse evidence a scan carries, **not** how corrupted it is (30%
outliers move it by under 2%, since they add a pedestal that normalizing by
the peak removes). It still correlates −0.46 with the vote's error, because
sparse returns are the failure that actually occurs.

### One frame error, caught by reading the diff

The vote's estimate and the command it's differenced against both live in
the *newest* frame (the bearing grid the scans were aligned into), but the
head's output doesn't: `translation()` adds it to a dead-reckoned
displacement in the body frame at the **start** of the tick. The residual
handed to the trunk was rotated by up to one tick of yaw, ~5°.

That's ~2 mm against an 11.5 mm correction limit at full turn rate — small
per tick, systematic across every turning step, correlated with exactly the
steps where slip is largest. The network receives gyro and could in
principle learn to undo it, but spending capacity relearning an exactly-known
rotation is what this design declines to do. Pinned by a geometric test: a
robot driving straight while turning must produce a purely forward residual;
the test checks itself against the counterfactual (undoing the rotation
should push the lateral component outside tolerance).

`belief_vote_v22`'s first run was discarded at step 780/1,955 once this was
found, rather than reported — measuring the buggy architecture wouldn't
have answered the question the run exists to answer.

### The result: causality up, mean down — and that is the whole finding

`belief_vote_v22`, 1,955 updates, scored beside every other estimator on the
paired offline evaluator in one invocation:

| checkpoint | final | drift/m | shuffled degradation | conf matched / shuffled |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v14 `iter_1130` | 8.56 cm | 2.51 | +0.68 cm | 0.133 / 0.083 |
| v19 control | 8.82 cm | 2.59 | +0.49 cm | 0.088 / 0.072 |
| v19 derotated | **8.53 cm** | 2.51 | +0.67 cm | 0.123 / 0.100 |
| v21 gate fix | 8.92 cm | 2.62 | +0.75 cm | 0.958 / 0.198 |
| **v22 vote** | 9.38 cm | 2.76 | **+0.96 cm** | **0.757 / 0.045** |
| dead reckoning | 10.71 cm | 3.15 | — | — |

On the mean the vote is **worst** of the five. On the number to read
instead — shuffled-lidar degradation — it's **best by 40%**, and the
confidence gate finally discriminates: 0.757 matched against 0.045
mismatched, a 17× separation where v14 managed 1.6×.

So the head is genuinely using the lidar more than any previous checkpoint,
and doing *worse*. Not a contradiction to explain away — it's the finding,
and it identifies the actual constraint.

## 2b. Why every encoder improvement has been worth nothing

Per-tick numbers as a *variance* statement, not just bias:

| | per-tick bias | per-tick scatter |
|---|---:|---:|
| command dead reckoning | +0.040 cm | 0.102 cm |
| consensus vote | −0.015 cm | 0.566 cm |

Over a 220-step episode, bias accumulates linearly and scatter as its
square root:

| estimator | systematic | random | total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| command only | 8.77 cm | 1.52 cm | **8.90 cm** |
| vote only | 3.24 cm | 8.40 cm | **9.00 cm** |
| optimal blend, α ≈ 0.5 | 2.81 cm | 4.24 cm | **5.08 cm** |

The vote used per tick is worth **exactly nothing** — its variance consumes
precisely the bias advantage it buys. Not a training failure, arithmetic —
and it predicts v22's result before the run.

**Filtering cannot rescue it.** Averaging the vote over a window cuts
per-sample variance but introduces correlation that exactly compensates in
the sum, and belief error is a sum — the integral of N noisy measurements
doesn't care how they were smoothed going in. Rules out
`persistent_calibration` and `motion_state_size` as the fix, which was the
obvious next thing to try.

### The loss has been asking for the wrong thing

The mechanism for four consecutive null results. Total loss:

```
20.0 * translation_loss  (per-tick, smooth_l1, normalized by V_MAX*DT)
 1.0 * belief_loss       (accumulated, squared, normalized by GOAL_TOLERANCE_M)
```

At the operating point these evaluate to ~**1.0 and 0.014** — the per-tick
term outweighs the integral it's a proxy for by **69×**. Per tick, the
command is the better estimator by a wide margin: inverse-variance weighting
of a 0.102 cm scatter against 0.566 cm gives the scan about **3% of the
weight**.

Gradient descent has been doing exactly the right thing for the objective it
was handed — the objective is wrong. Every encoder improvement made scan
evidence *more available*; none changed the fact that using it costs
per-tick accuracy. The head converged to a better command rescaling every
time, which is precisely what yesterday's ablation measured (head reading
`prev_action` 4.6× more than gyro) without identifying why.

v22 is the confirming case — the first checkpoint where the gate has real
dynamic range and the head actually leans on the lidar, and the first where
the mean got materially worse. Forced to use the scan under a per-tick loss,
it does the thing the loss rewards least.

### The integral-weighted loss works, on the axis it was aimed at

`belief_integral_v23` — same config as v22 with `--translation-coef 2
--belief-coef 20`, ~3× in favour of the integral:

| checkpoint | final | drift/m | shuffled degradation | conf matched / shuffled |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v19 derotated | **8.53 cm** | 2.51 | +0.67 cm | 0.123 / 0.100 |
| v22 vote, per-tick loss | 9.38 cm | 2.76 | +0.96 cm | 0.757 / 0.045 |
| **v23 vote, integral loss** | 8.73 cm | 2.57 | **+1.80 cm** | 0.600 / 0.025 |
| dead reckoning | 10.71 cm | 3.15 | — | — |

Reweighting recovered most of what forcing the vote in had cost (9.38 →
8.73) and **more than doubled lidar causality again** — 1.80 cm against
v22's 0.96 and 0.67 for the best checkpoint without the vote. Removing the
lidar sends error from 8.73 to 10.53 cm, 91% of the way back to dead
reckoning. For the first time in this project the estimator is genuinely
lidar-driven rather than a command rescaling. The mean still doesn't beat
the best baseline (8.73 vs 8.53) — §2c is why, and it's not something more
training fixes.

## 2c. How much scan-based ego-motion this sensor actually supports

The `sqrt(S/L)` scaling says the way to cut belief error is a longer
baseline — matching over 40 cm instead of 2.3 cm should buy 4×. The
per-tick vote can't do it (its range-flow model is first order and assumes
equal-bearing correspondence), but a correlative scan matcher has neither
limitation: works in Cartesian space against a blurred likelihood field, so
displacement is exact and no bearing correspondence is assumed, while
outliers still abstain under every candidate.

`scripts/probe_correlative_match.py` implements it, validated on analytic
rooms to recover 30 cm displacements to **0.3 cm**, including under 1.2 rad
of simultaneous rotation.

On corpus scans it fails completely — 58 cm error on a 40 cm baseline. So:
**does the true displacement score better than no displacement at all?**

| baseline | travel | truth outscores zero | median score ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 tick | 2 cm | **97%** of windows | 1.12 |
| 4 ticks | 9 cm | 43% | 0.95 |
| 9 ticks | 21 cm | 41% | 0.91 |
| 19 ticks | 40 cm | 44% | 0.88 |

At one tick the motion is unambiguously present, why the vote works. By
four ticks it's gone: a *static* alignment explains the newer scan better
than the true one, in the majority of windows. Not an estimator failure —
measured on the score surface itself, no search involved.

That closes the `sqrt(L)` route. Information decays with baseline faster
than the longer lever recovers, so per-tick isn't a choice but the only
regime available, and the per-tick variance floor (0.566 cm) bounds what any
frame-to-frame estimator can achieve here. Combined with §2b: a perfectly
weighted per-tick blend lands near 5 cm, the ceiling of this approach.

Why the information decays so fast is open, with a concrete suspect: the
frames aren't snapshots — `sweep_scan` stitches each one from five stored
ticks and **quantizes each beam's acquisition time to the nearest tick**,
costed by its own docstring at "half a control period, or 25 ms." At
0.46 m/s that's 1.15 cm of unmodelled per-beam position error — the same
size as the per-tick signal, and it doesn't cancel between frames.
`LIDAR_SPIN_HZ = 5.5` is also still unverified against the A1. Both are
sensor-reconstruction properties rather than estimator properties, where the
next look belongs, along with local-map accumulation (whose purpose is to
average per-frame stitching error away).

The blend arithmetic sets the ceiling honestly: even a perfectly weighted
combination lands at 5.08 cm, right at the gate's 5 cm threshold. Beating it
needs the vote's *scatter* down, not its bias — to ≤0.46 cm for 5 cm of
belief error, ≤0.27 cm for 3 cm. Grid resolution isn't the limit (scatter is
0.56 cm at 21, 31, and 41 bins alike).

## 3. The declared uncertainty was not describing the error

On hardware the belief is the only arrival signal there is. The robot stops
when its own upper confidence bound on remaining distance falls inside
`arrival_radius_m`, so a covariance that understates the error doesn't
produce a cautious robot that stops late — it produces a confident one that
stops in the wrong place and reports success.

`scripts/probe_arrival_honesty.py` on `iter_1130.pt`, 1,383 scored steps:

| quantity | measured |
|---|---:|
| mean belief error | 3.01 cm |
| mean declared 1-sigma | 0.30 cm |
| **error / declared sigma** | **8.76** |
| **coverage at 1.96 sigma** | **9.5%** (nominal 95%) |
| error growth with travel | 3.56 cm/m |
| declared sigma growth with travel | 0.32 cm/m |

The last two rows are the diagnosis. `belief_losses` calibrates the
variance head against *per-step* squared translation error and
`_propagate_covariance` sums those independently down the episode — a
random walk, growing as the square root of step count. The dominant error is
a systematic wheel-scale bias, growing linearly in distance. The declared
bound falls behind the true error by roughly the square root of episode
length, silently. No amount of retraining the variance head fixes this —
it's the wrong model form.

### The fix, and what it costs

`drift_scale_uncertainty` adds the systematic term, carrying cumulative path
length as one more float of explicit state. The variance increment across a
step is `eps^2 * ((s + ds)^2 - s^2)` — writing it as a difference of
squares rather than `eps^2 * ds^2` is the whole point, since the cross term
`2 s ds` is what makes the error accumulate linearly instead of as a random
walk.

| `drift_scale_uncertainty` | error / declared σ | 1.96σ coverage |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.0 (every existing checkpoint) | 8.76 | 9.5% |
| 0.03 | 1.46 | 78.3% |
| **0.05** | **0.96** | **94.2%** |

0.05 is calibrated for the current estimator.

**Be clear what an honest bound buys and doesn't.** It widens by
`1.96 * 0.05 * s`, 49 cm after five metres — far outside the 15 cm arrival
radius. A calibrated robot therefore *declines to declare arrival* on a long
episode rather than declaring it wrongly, converting false arrivals into
timeouts — the right trade for hardware. It does not manufacture accuracy:
reaching the 85% correct-arrival gate needs an estimator that drifts less,
which is what §2 is for; the two changes are complementary, not
alternatives.

Default is 0.0, so every existing checkpoint keeps its exact behaviour, and
a checkpoint that declares a drift term refuses to run without the carried
distance rather than silently restoring the dishonest bound.

## 4. Phase 5 exists now, in part

`scripts/export_belief.py` exports the deployable step through
`torch.export` — the exact front end `to_edge_transform_and_lower`
consumes — and fails if the exported program doesn't reproduce the eager
module on adversarial state: non-trivial belief, inflated covariance, a
partly elapsed arrival count, and a scan with 25% missing returns.

On `iter_1130.pt`: **41 distinct operators, 491,159 parameters, parity
exactly 0.0, 0.69 ms median host-CPU latency** against a 50 ms control
period.

### The vote stays out of the exported graph, and that had to be built

Started as a comment rather than a mechanism, and running the script is what
exposed it. The first version "removed" the vote by setting the module to
`None`, which doesn't work on a checkpoint trained with one — the trunk's
first layer is sized to include the vote's features, so it raises a shape
error rather than degrading. A docstring claim the code doesn't support is
worse than no claim.

`compute_vote` is now a real entry point and `step_state` takes an optional
precomputed vote, so the network traces with the vote's outputs as inputs:

| | operators | host latency |
|---|---:|---:|
| vote outside the graph | 50 | 1.17 ms |
| vote traced in | 56 | 2.46 ms |

The extra six are `gather`, `cumsum`, `flip`, `remainder`, `floor`, and a
`set_grad_enabled` higher-order wrapper, none proven delegated to HTP by the
reference models. It's also an argmax over a consensus surface measuring
centimetres, and INT8 across the candidate grid is about 1 mm — quantizing a
quantity whose whole content is the sharpness of one peak degrades without
erroring.

**The seam is the parameter boundary, not the vote branch.** An earlier
version put the convolution that reads the surface on the preprocessing
side, which would have run learned weights in float on the CPU and quietly
spread the model across the quantization boundary. `_vote_surfaces` is
parameter-free geometry; `_encode_vote` has weights and stays in the graph,
with the surface crossing as a small image.

A test requires the precomputed path to be bit-identical to computing the
vote inline — the `rollout`-versus-`step` bug class, where a divergence
would appear only as an unexplained sim-to-real gap. The vote itself costs
0.42 ms single-threaded at 21 bins and 0.86 ms at 31, so the budget isn't in
question.

Still unbuilt: INT8 calibration from the final policy's own state
distribution, the QNN lowering, the C++ node, and the preprocessing parity
test against it. `archive/v1-numpy-pipeline` has a working version of all
four.

## 6. The ceiling is the sensor reconstruction

Everything above chases an estimator. The estimator was never the
constraint.

> **Read §7 with this section.** The figures below came from an instrument
> whose search extent was capped, and the mechanism proposed at the end of
> this section — beam-age quantization — was implemented and **refuted**.
> The conclusion that the ceiling lives in the reconstruction survives; the
> numbers and cause are corrected there.

### The measurement

Take two reconstructed scan frames. Place the older one using the **true**
relative pose — not the command, not an estimate, ground truth from
`batch.belief` — then ask how far the best alignment still sits from zero.
A perfect sensor reconstruction would answer zero.

| | 0× sensor noise | 1× sensor noise |
|---|---:|---:|
| baseline 1 tick | 0.665 cm | 0.629 cm |
| baseline 4 ticks | 7.81 cm | 7.13 cm |

*(Superseded — §7 measures 1.095 / 8.560 cm at 0× with a corrected search.)*

Two things to notice. **Sensor noise is irrelevant** — the 0× and 1×
columns agree; this is the reconstruction disagreeing with itself, not
range noise. **The 4-tick figure is the sweep period**: `LIDAR_SPIN_HZ =
5.5` gives a 0.182 s revolution, 3.6 control ticks, and full-speed travel in
one revolution is **8.36 cm** — the measured 4-tick inconsistency is 7.8 cm.
Frames more than one sweep period apart are stitched from *disjoint*
sub-scan sets, and their geometric distortions don't cancel.

That coincidence is what suggested beam-age quantization as the cause — the
wrong inference: §7 implements sub-tick placement and it changes nothing,
so the magnitude match isn't evidence of the mechanism.

### Why this ends the argument

The quantity the estimator must measure is the slip residual: **0.078 cm
per tick**. The reconstruction's own frame-to-frame inconsistency is
**1.095 cm** at one tick and **8.56 cm** at four (§7's corrected figures).
Noise is 14× the signal at the only baseline where matching works at all,
110× at the baseline that would otherwise carry enough signal.

No estimator can recover a quantity an order of magnitude below the noise
floor of its own input. That single fact explains, without further
hypothesis: three architectures plateauing at the same held-out error
(2026-08-01); a point-to-line solver and full ICP recovering nothing while
exact on analytic rooms (2026-08-02); derotation, the scaled difference
channel, and the gate fix each correctly diagnosed, correctly implemented,
worth nothing (§1, 2026-08-02); the consensus vote recovering the slip
residual with correlation **0.03** while correlating 0.92 with truth
(because truth is nearly the command already, §2b); observability
collapsing between baseline 1 and baseline 4 (§2c) — the sweep period, not a
property of rooms; scan-to-map failing too (correlation 0.02), since
averaging ten frames can't fix a distortion that's per-frame and
systematic. Every one of those was read as an estimator or encoder problem.
None were.

### What it means for the robot

The conclusion that survives §7 is the negative one. **Survives:** this
corpus cannot answer whether lidar can measure ego-motion on this robot —
its reconstruction disagrees with itself by an order of magnitude more than
the signal, so every architectural conclusion drawn from it about
scan-based localization describes the sensor model rather than the sensor.

**Does not survive:** the specific claim, made here before §7 was measured,
that `sweep_scan`'s 25 ms age rounding is the cause and that a real A1's
per-return timestamps therefore give hardware "strictly better
information." Sub-tick placement is implemented in §7 and changes nothing —
the rounding, while a real approximation, isn't what's costing the 1.1 cm.
Whether hardware is better here is now open rather than concluded, and
stays open until the actual cause is found.

## 7. The sub-tick fix: implemented, measured, and it does not work

§6 diagnosed the ceiling as beam-age quantization in `sweep_scan` and
prescribed sub-tick reconstruction. It's now implemented, tested, measured
— and the diagnosis was **wrong**.

### The instrument, corrected first

`scripts/probe_sensor_consistency.py` places one frame into another using
the true relative pose and measures the residual. Its first version
searched a fixed ±10 cm, silently capping the answer once the baseline
exceeds ~4 ticks and reporting the *travel* as if it were reconstruction
error. Validated on analytic rooms with a baseline-scaled search, the
matcher's own floor is flat:

| baseline | matcher floor on analytic rooms |
|---|---:|
| 1 tick | 0.21 cm |
| 4 ticks | 0.24 cm |
| 9 ticks | 0.27 cm |

The §6 figures (0.665 / 7.81 cm) came from the capped instrument and a
smaller sample. Corrected, search scaled to baseline:

| baseline | nearest-tick (current) | sub-tick (new) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 tick, 0× noise | **1.095 cm** | 1.151 cm |
| 4 ticks, 0× noise | **8.560 cm** | 9.251 cm |
| 9 ticks, 0× noise | **23.045 cm** | 22.056 cm |
| 1 tick, 1× noise | 0.862 cm | 0.934 cm |
| 4 ticks, 1× noise | 9.051 cm | 8.300 cm |

Widening the search made the numbers *worse*, the right sign: the score
surface genuinely has its optimum far from the truth, rather than the
search being unable to reach it.

### What survives, and what does not

**Survives.** The ceiling is real and in the reconstruction, not the
estimator. At one tick it's 1.1 cm against a 0.078 cm/tick slip signal —
**14× the quantity any estimator must measure**, 5× the matcher's own
floor. Not sensor noise: 0× and 1× columns agree throughout.

**Does not survive.** Beam-age quantization isn't the cause. Sub-tick
reconstruction — interpolating both range and motion correction at each
beam's true acquisition time, kept consistent — changes nothing and is
marginally worse. The 25 ms rounding was a real approximation and not the
binding one.

The code stays, off by default, tested, honestly labelled — correct and
cheap, just not the constraint. Same disposition `--derotate-scan-history`
earned on 2026-08-02, for the same reason: fixing a defect that isn't the
bottleneck buys nothing, and recording the measurement is what stops it
being retried.

### The next candidate, with its arithmetic

`rasterize_lidar` snaps every deskewed return back onto a fixed 720-bin
angular grid. Deskew moves beams to irregular bearings; the grid quantizes
them again. At the corpus's median valid range of 1.23 m, one 0.5° bin
subtends 1.08 cm of arc, so uniform quantization contributes **0.311 cm
RMS** — about a third of the measured 1.095 cm floor, already 4× the slip
signal on its own.

A *representation* fix rather than a sensor fix, and the cheaper of the two:
keep the deskewed `(range, exact bearing)` pairs instead of re-binning onto
a grid — the estimator's Cartesian point cloud never needed the grid, only
the actor's 180-bin observation does. It accounts for a third, not all, so
it's a candidate, not a diagnosis. Ruled out so far, by measurement: sensor
noise, beam-age quantization, and search failure in the instrument.
