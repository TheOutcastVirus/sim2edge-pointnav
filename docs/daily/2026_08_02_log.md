# 2026-08-02 — Why the estimator plateaus: two encoder defects and one that matters

Yesterday's handoff called the plateau "a localization observability/
modeling ceiling." Today tested that claim directly instead of accepting it.

The ceiling is **not** observability — scans contain the motion: median
range change between consecutive reconstructed frames tracks distance
travelled at ratio 1.07, at zero sensor noise. What's missing is a mechanism
able to extract it from data where a third of bearings are gross outliers.

Two concrete encoder defects were found. One was fixed and **made no
difference**, itself the useful result. The other is a genuine bug in how
the confidence gate is trained, explaining the constant 0.13 every
checkpoint reports.

---

## 1. The scan history is never rotationally aligned

`OdometryHead._features` stacks 20 frames of 720-bin scans as convolution
channels, each in its own body frame — nothing removes the yaw between
them, so the convolution compares *different bearings* whenever the robot
turns. Measured yaw from oldest to newest frame of the one-second window,
on `belief_onpolicy_v15`:

| | mean | p50 | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| radians | 0.386 | 0.265 | 1.198 | 1.729 |
| **shift, of 720 bins** | **44.2** | 30.3 | **137.3** | 198.1 |

The encoder's receptive field is ~28 bins (`Conv1d(k=7,s=2)` then
`Conv1d(k=5,s=2)` → 15, widened by `AdaptiveAvgPool1d(24)`). **The median
rotation already exceeds it** — content a cross-frame comparison needs is
usually outside the window that could match it. The exact resampler needed,
`OdometryHead._rotate_scan`, already existed at line 250: dead code,
defined, never called, untested.

## 2. Derotation, implemented and measured: no effect

`derotate_scan_history` rotates every frame into the newest frame's bearing
grid using cumulative gyro, reusing `_rotate_scan`; convention matches
`deskew_history` and the live evaluator's FIFO, so it transfers to
deployment unchanged. Off by default — every existing checkpoint keeps its
exact numerics.

Pinned by a geometric test: a robot turning in place through 1.1 rad sees
every range change, but after alignment each frame must reproduce the
newest one. Raw spread >0.5 m; aligned spread <5 mm (linear-interpolation
error on a 0.5° grid). A second test fixed a latent `_rotate_scan` bug — its
mask required both interpolation neighbours, eroding the newest frame's own
mask at zero rotation.

Fresh head on `belief_onpolicy_v15` from v14, 1,955 updates, against the
identical head without derotation, scored on the paired offline evaluator
(previously quoted numbers used different batch sizes and aren't
comparable):

| checkpoint | final mean | p95 | drift/m | vs DR | shuffled degradation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v14 `iter_1130` (existing) | 8.56 cm | 20.75 cm | 2.51 cm/m | +2.15 cm | 0.68 cm |
| v19 derotated | **8.53 cm** | 20.57 cm | 2.51 cm/m | +2.18 cm | 0.67 cm |

**0.03 cm.** Identical drift, identical causality. Dead reckoning is
10.71 cm here — both remove 20% of it, neither does better. A same-config
control (fresh head, same LR, derotation off) separated the flag from
reinitialization, scored on the trainer's own held-out validation (a
noisier instrument, not comparable to the paired evaluator above):

| run | validation final | p95 | drift/m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| v19 control (no derotation) | 8.76 cm | 18.13 cm | 2.62 cm/m |
| v19 derotated | **8.51 cm** | 17.34 cm | 2.56 cm/m |

0.25 cm in the right direction on one seed, against the paired evaluator's
0.03 cm: derotation is worth somewhere between nothing and a quarter
centimetre, and the gates are 3.5 cm away. Not the constraint. The §1
measurement wasn't wrong — the encoder was never performing cross-frame
matching in the first place, so removing an obstacle to matching changed
nothing: it regresses from 24 pooled sector summaries per frame, and at
30 bins/sector that representation can't carry a 2 cm parallax whether or
not frames are aligned. Keep the flag — cheap, correct, a prerequisite for
any encoder that does attempt correspondence — but not itself an
improvement.

## 3. The confidence gate is starved, and 0.13 is its initialization

Yesterday's reading was that the gate settles near 0.13 because the
proposal is only 13% trustworthy. Wrong. The correction is
`confidence * proposal`; proposal head inits at `uniform(-1e-3, 1e-3)`,
gate at `sigmoid(-2.0) = 0.119`. So the proposal's gradient carries a
factor of the gate (0.119, healthy) while the gate's gradient carries a
factor of the *proposal* (~1e-3, vanishing). Measured inside the real
training loss: the gate's gradient is **741× smaller** than the proposal
head's. Raising the gate's initial bias doesn't help — it lifts both by the
same sigmoid factor.

Gradient descent satisfies the loss by growing the proposal instead — to
~8× the residual (proposal `tanh` magnitude 0.31 against a 1.15 cm
per-tick limit is 0.36 cm, against a residual of ~0.045 cm) — while the
gate sits where it started: v14 gate bias −2.008 against init −2.0, and
over the v19 run the mean gate *drifted down*, 0.119→0.073.

This also corrects yesterday's read of the `k` sweep (constant multiplier
on the gate: 0.8→9.483 cm, 1.0→9.494, 2.0→12.197, 4.0→25.842, optimal at
`k=1`) as evidence of correct shrinkage — it isn't: the sweep only sees
that the *product* is calibrated, and can't distinguish a correctly small
gate from a correspondingly large proposal. The gate has no dynamic range
at all; it's a constant, so the model can't express "this scan is
informative" vs "this one is not" — the one thing a gate is for. Overlap
isn't the constraint either: the hard observability gate
`(overlap/0.5).clamp(0,1)` is saturated at 1.0 for 100% of samples.

### The fix

The counterfactual `mismatched_lidar` already builds supplies paired
labels for exactly the gate's question — *is this scan evidence about my
motion?* `confidence_coef` adds a hinge on the separation between a step's
matched and mismatched gate values, reaching the gate without passing
through the proposal (gradient O(1), scales linearly with the coefficient:
measured 0.117→0.72→3.62 at coefficients 0, 1, 5). Being a hinge rather than
a target, it asserts no absolute confidence — a genuinely uninformative
real scan is free to score low, it must merely outrank the mismatched scan.

## 4. The scans do contain the motion

Built a geometric estimator with no learned component to test observability
directly: gyro-derotate then point-to-line solve for translation, and
separately a full ICP with Cartesian nearest-neighbour correspondence. Both
validated against analytic circular/square rooms first (caught a sign error
that would otherwise look like a null result):

| room | range noise | truth | recovered | error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| circle | 2 cm | 10.0 cm | 10.00 cm | 0.20 cm |
| box (flat walls) | 2 cm | 10.0 cm | 10.02 cm | 0.32 cm |
| circle | 2 cm | **0.08 cm** | 0.14 cm | 0.19 cm |
| box | 2 cm | **0.08 cm** | 0.15 cm | 0.30 cm |

Forward precision on a one-tick slip signal is ~0.07 cm at realistic noise
— close to the 0.075 cm floor 2 cm of range noise over 720 beams implies.
If per-interval estimates at that precision were unbiased, accumulating
over an episode would put belief error near 1 cm rather than 8.5.

**On corpus scans both solvers fail completely** at zero sensor noise: ICP
reports −1.5 ± 9.1 cm where the robot moved +10.4 cm, correlation +0.089.
Looked at first like a broken reconstruction (mean range difference between
consecutive frames is 13.3 cm for 2 cm of travel), but it's a
mean-vs-median artefact — the **median**:

| gap | travel | median \|Δr\| |
|---|---:|---:|
| straight, fast | 2.25 cm | 2.15 cm |
| turning hard | 1.13 cm | 1.14 cm |
| all ticks | 1.99 cm | 2.13 cm |

Ratio ≈ 1.0 — **frames are consistent rigid views, motion is present in
them.** The heavy tail is depth edges in cluttered rooms: mean is 6× the
median, so roughly a third of bearings are gross outliers. That's the real
difficulty, not observability — the synthetic rooms that validated both
solvers have no clutter or occlusion changes, so never tested this failure
mode; neither solver's robust weighting survives ~30% breakdown with active
mis-association. Sensor noise is irrelevant (every number here is at
`noise_scale 0.0`; noisy figures differ by under 1 cm).

## 5. What is built but not yet measured

Two changes implemented and tested but with **no training result behind
them** — neither should be called an improvement yet:

* `scaled_scan_difference` — masks the cross-frame range difference to
  bearings valid in both frames, scales by the translation a window can
  contain rather than `LIDAR_MAX_M`. `belief_derot_scaled_v20` was stopped
  by hand at step 820/~1,900; `latest.pt` holds step 800, a partial run,
  not a result.
* `confidence_coef` — the §3 fix. `belief_gate_v21` was queued, never
  started.

To finish, in order (~20 min each on a free card, not parallelizable — one
run holds ~16 GB):

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/train_belief.py \
  --data data/belief_onpolicy_v15 \
  --belief-start runs/belief_ppo_biasfix_v14/iter_1130.pt \
  --out runs/belief_gate_v21 \
  --epochs 1 --batch-size 8 --learning-rate 3e-4 \
  --derotate-scan-history --scaled-scan-difference \
  --confidence-coef 20 --confidence-gate-bias 0.0 \
  --reset-odometry --checkpoint-every 200 --seed 0
```

Then score everything on one instrument (§2's numbers are only comparable
because they came from a single invocation):

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_belief_offline.py \
  runs/belief_ppo_biasfix_v14/iter_1130.pt \
  runs/belief_control_v19/final.pt \
  runs/belief_derot_v19/final.pt \
  runs/belief_gate_v21/final.pt \
  --data data/belief_onpolicy_v15 --batch-size 8 --max-batches 32 \
  --out runs/encoder_ablation.json
```

Read `shuffled_lidar_degradation_m`, not the mean. Both existing checkpoints
sit at 0.67–0.68 cm; if the gate fix works it should rise, since dynamic
range is what lets the head reject a scan rather than average it in. A mean
that improves while shuffled degradation stays flat means the head found a
better command rescaling — what the last three runs did.

## 6. Status

Do not describe the ceiling as observability. The defensible statement:

* the corpus supports scan-based ego-motion to ~0.07 cm per interval in
  clean geometry;
* real scenes present ~30% gross outliers, and no estimator tried so far —
  learned or geometric — is robust to that;
* the learned head is additionally unable to express confidence, precisely
  the quantity that would let it down-weight those outliers.

No end-to-end number moved today. Best closed-loop result is still
yesterday's 75.8% navigation success / 72.7% correct declared arrival /
11.5 cm mean, against gates of 85% / 85% / 5 cm; no closed-loop evaluation
ran today.

Order of work: (1) finish the two §5 runs — the gate fix is the only change
today with a diagnosed mechanism and no measurement yet; (2) replace the
24-sector average pooling, the best explanation for why derotation bought

rather than by yesterday's observability argument, which §4 contradicts.
