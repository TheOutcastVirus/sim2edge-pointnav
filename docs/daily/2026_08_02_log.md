# 2026-08-02 — Why the estimator plateaus: two encoder defects and one that matters

Yesterday's handoff said three increasingly expressive estimators had landed on
the same number and called it "a localization observability/modeling ceiling."
Today tested that claim directly rather than accepting it.

The ceiling is **not** observability. The scans contain the motion: the median
range change between consecutive reconstructed frames tracks the distance
travelled at a ratio of 1.07, at zero sensor noise. What is missing is a
mechanism able to extract it from data where a third of bearings are gross
outliers.

Two concrete defects were found in the encoder. One was fixed and **made no
difference**, which is itself the useful result. The other is a genuine bug in
how the confidence gate is trained, and it explains the constant 0.13 every
checkpoint reports.

---

## 1. The scan history is never rotationally aligned

`OdometryHead._features` stacks 20 frames of 720-bin scans as convolution
channels. Each frame sits in its own body frame. Nothing removes the yaw between
them, so the convolution compares *different bearings* of the scene whenever the
robot turns.

Measured on `belief_onpolicy_v15`, yaw from the oldest to the newest frame of the
one-second window:

| | mean | p50 | p95 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| radians | 0.386 | 0.265 | 1.198 | 1.729 |
| **shift, of 720 bins** | **44.2** | 30.3 | **137.3** | 198.1 |

The encoder's receptive field is about 28 bins: `Conv1d(k=7,s=2)` then
`Conv1d(k=5,s=2)` gives 15, and the `AdaptiveAvgPool1d(24)` that follows widens it
to roughly 28. **The median rotation already exceeds it.** Content that a
cross-frame comparison would need to match is outside the window that could match
it, most of the time.

`OdometryHead._rotate_scan` — the exact resampler this needs — was already in the
file at line 250. It was dead code: defined, never called, untested.

## 2. Derotation, implemented and measured: no effect

`derotate_scan_history` rotates every frame into the newest frame's bearing grid
using the cumulative gyro, reusing `_rotate_scan`. The gyro convention matches
`deskew_history` ("entry `s` describes the motion ending at stored scan `s`") and
the live evaluator's FIFO, so it transfers to deployment unchanged. Off by
default; every existing checkpoint keeps its exact numerics.

Correctness is pinned by a geometric test rather than a shape check: a robot
turning in place through 1.1 rad sees every range change, but after alignment each
frame must reproduce the newest one. Raw spread across the history is >0.5 m;
aligned spread is <5 mm, which is linear-interpolation error on a 0.5° grid. A
second test fixes a latent bug in `_rotate_scan` — its mask required both
interpolation neighbours, which eroded the newest frame's own mask at zero
rotation.

A fresh odometry head trained on `belief_onpolicy_v15` from the v14 actor, 1,955
updates, against the identical head without derotation. Both scored by the paired
offline evaluator at identical settings, because previously quoted numbers came
from different batch sizes and are not comparable:

| checkpoint | final mean | p95 | drift/m | vs DR | shuffled degradation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v14 `iter_1130` (existing) | 8.56 cm | 20.75 cm | 2.51 cm/m | +2.15 cm | 0.68 cm |
| v19 derotated | **8.53 cm** | 20.57 cm | 2.51 cm/m | +2.18 cm | 0.67 cm |

**0.03 cm.** Identical drift, identical causality. Dead reckoning is 10.71 cm on
this instrument, so both remove 20% of it and neither does better.

A same-configuration control — fresh head, same learning rate, derotation off —
was then run to separate the flag from the reinitialization. Scored by the
trainer's own held-out validation, which is a different and noisier instrument
than the paired evaluator above and must not be compared across the two:

| run | validation final | p95 | drift/m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| v19 control (no derotation) | 8.76 cm | 18.13 cm | 2.62 cm/m |
| v19 derotated | **8.51 cm** | 17.34 cm | 2.56 cm/m |

0.25 cm, in the right direction, on one seed. Read against the paired evaluator's
0.03 cm, the honest summary is that derotation is worth somewhere between nothing
and a quarter of a centimetre, and that the gates are 3.5 cm away. It is not the
constraint.

The conclusion is not that the measurement in §1 was wrong. It is that the
encoder was never performing cross-frame matching in the first place, so removing
an obstacle to matching changed nothing. The architecture regresses from 24
pooled sector summaries per frame; at 30 bins per sector that representation
cannot carry a 2 cm parallax whether or not the frames are aligned.

Keep the flag. It is cheap, correct, and a prerequisite for any encoder that does
attempt correspondence. It is not by itself an improvement.

## 3. The confidence gate is starved, and 0.13 is its initialization

Yesterday's §5-equivalent reading was that the gate settles near 0.13 because the
proposal is only 13% trustworthy. That is wrong, and the mechanism matters.

The correction is `confidence * proposal`. The proposal head is initialized at
`uniform(-1e-3, 1e-3)`; the gate at `sigmoid(-2.0) = 0.119`. So:

* the proposal's gradient carries a factor of the gate — 0.119, healthy;
* the gate's gradient carries a factor of the *proposal* — ~1e-3, vanishing.

Measured inside the real training loss, with the mismatched-lidar term already
included, the gate's gradient is **741× smaller** than the proposal head's.
Raising the gate's initial bias does not help: it lifts both by the same sigmoid
factor, and at bias 0.0 the ratio is if anything worse.

So gradient descent satisfies the loss by growing the proposal instead. It grows
it to roughly 8× the residual — measured proposal `tanh` magnitude 0.31 against a
1.15 cm per-tick limit is 0.36 cm, against a residual of ~0.045 cm — while the
gate sits where it started. Confirmed across runs: the v14 gate bias is −2.008
against an init of −2.0, and over the v19 run the mean gate *drifted down*, 0.119
→ 0.073.

This also corrects a measurement from yesterday. Sweeping a constant multiplier
`k` on the gate found `k=1` optimal (0.8 → 9.483 cm, 1.0 → 9.494, 2.0 → 12.197,
4.0 → 25.842) and that was read as evidence of correct shrinkage. It is not: the
sweep can only see that the *product* is calibrated, and cannot distinguish a
correctly small gate from a correspondingly large proposal. The gate has no
dynamic range at all — it is a constant, so the model cannot express "this scan is
informative" versus "this one is not," which is the one thing a gate is for.

Overlap is not the constraint either. The hard observability gate
`(overlap/0.5).clamp(0,1)` is **saturated at 1.0 for 100% of samples**; consecutive
sweeps share valid returns 86.4% of the time.

### The fix

The counterfactual the `mismatched_lidar` term already builds supplies paired
labels for exactly the gate's question — *is this scan evidence about my motion?*
`confidence_coef` adds a hinge on the separation between a step's matched and
mismatched gate values. It reaches the gate without passing through the proposal,
so its gradient is O(1) and scales linearly with the coefficient (measured: gate
gradient 0.117 → 0.72 → 3.62 at coefficients 0, 1, 5). Being a hinge rather than a
target, it asserts no absolute confidence: a genuinely uninformative real scan is
free to score low, it must merely outrank the same step's mismatched scan.

## 4. The scans do contain the motion

The observability claim deserved a direct test, so a geometric estimator was built
with no learned component: gyro-derotate, then a point-to-line solve for
translation only, and separately a full ICP with Cartesian nearest-neighbour
correspondence. Both were validated against analytic circular and square rooms
first, which caught a sign error that would otherwise have looked like a null
result:

| room | range noise | truth | recovered | error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| circle | 2 cm | 10.0 cm | 10.00 cm | 0.20 cm |
| box (flat walls) | 2 cm | 10.0 cm | 10.02 cm | 0.32 cm |
| circle | 2 cm | **0.08 cm** | 0.14 cm | 0.19 cm |
| box | 2 cm | **0.08 cm** | 0.15 cm | 0.30 cm |

Forward precision on a one-tick slip signal is ~0.07 cm at realistic noise —
about the 0.075 cm floor that 2 cm of range noise over 720 beams implies. If
per-interval estimates at that precision were unbiased, accumulating them over an
episode would put belief error near 1 cm rather than 8.5.

**On corpus scans both solvers fail completely**, at zero sensor noise: ICP
reports −1.5 ± 9.1 cm where the robot moved +10.4 cm, correlation +0.089.

That looked at first like the reconstruction being broken, and the *mean*
range difference between consecutive frames supports it — 13.3 cm for 2 cm of
travel. It is a mean-versus-median artefact. The **median**:

| gap | travel | median \|Δr\| |
|---|---:|---:|
| straight, fast | 2.25 cm | 2.15 cm |
| turning hard | 1.13 cm | 1.14 cm |
| all ticks | 1.99 cm | 2.13 cm |

Ratio ≈ 1.0. **The frames are consistent rigid views and the motion is present in
them.** The heavy tail is depth edges in cluttered rooms, and it is large: the
mean is 6× the median, so roughly a third of bearings are gross outliers.

That is the real difficulty, and it is not observability. The synthetic rooms that
validated both solvers have no clutter and no occlusion changes, so they never
tested the failure mode. Neither solver's robust weighting survives ~30% breakdown
with active mis-association. Sensor noise is irrelevant here — every number in
this section is at `noise_scale 0.0`, and the noisy figures differ by under 1 cm.

## 5. What is built but not yet measured

Two changes are implemented and tested but have **no training result behind them**.
Neither should be described as an improvement until one exists.

* `scaled_scan_difference` — masks the cross-frame range difference to bearings
  valid in both frames and scales it by the translation a window can contain
  rather than by `LIDAR_MAX_M`. The channel currently carries a centimetre signal
  divided by 12 m, three orders below the absolute-range channel beside it, and
  pairs a real hit against a max-range fill to manufacture a 9 m "closure." Run
  `belief_derot_scaled_v20` was stopped by hand at step 820 of ~1,900;
  `runs/belief_derot_scaled_v20/latest.pt` holds step 800 and is a partial run,
  not a result.
* `confidence_coef` — the §3 gate fix. Run `belief_gate_v21` was queued and never
  started.

To finish, in this order (each ~20 min on a free card, and they cannot be
parallelized — one run holds ~16 GB):

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/train_belief.py \
  --data data/belief_onpolicy_v15 \
  --belief-start runs/belief_ppo_biasfix_v14/iter_1130.pt \
  --out runs/belief_gate_v21 \
  --epochs 1 --batch-size 8 --learning-rate 3e-4 \
  --derotate-scan-history --scaled-scan-difference \
  --confidence-coef 20 --confidence-gate-bias 0.0 \
  --reset-odometry --checkpoint-every 200 --seed 0
```

Then score everything on one instrument — the numbers in §2 are only comparable
because they came from a single invocation:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_belief_offline.py \
  runs/belief_ppo_biasfix_v14/iter_1130.pt \
  runs/belief_control_v19/final.pt \
  runs/belief_derot_v19/final.pt \
  runs/belief_gate_v21/final.pt \
  --data data/belief_onpolicy_v15 --batch-size 8 --max-batches 32 \
  --out runs/encoder_ablation.json
```

The number to read is `shuffled_lidar_degradation_m`, not the mean. Both existing
checkpoints sit at 0.67–0.68 cm; if the gate fix works it should rise, because a
gate with dynamic range is what lets the head reject a scan rather than average it
in. A mean that improves while shuffled degradation stays flat is the head finding
a better command rescaling, which is what the last three runs did.

## 6. Status

Do not describe the ceiling as observability. The defensible statement is:

* the corpus supports scan-based ego-motion to roughly 0.07 cm per interval in
  clean geometry;
* real scenes present ~30% gross outliers, and no estimator tried so far —
  learned or geometric — is robust to that;
* the learned head is additionally unable to express confidence, which is
  precisely the quantity that would let it down-weight those outliers.

No end-to-end number moved today. The best closed-loop result is still yesterday's
75.8% navigation success / 72.7% correct declared arrival / 11.5 cm mean, against
gates of 85% / 85% / 5 cm, and no closed-loop evaluation was run today.

Order of work:

1. Finish the two runs in §5. The gate fix is the only change today with a
   diagnosed mechanism behind it and no measurement yet.
2. Replace the 24-sector average pooling. It is the best explanation for why
   derotation bought nothing: 30 bins per sector cannot carry a 2 cm parallax, so
   no correspondence-like computation survives it, aligned or not.
3. Only then consider a local-map accumulation. It is the standard answer to
   outlier-heavy matching, and §4 now gives it a quantitative justification —
   ~30% gross outliers — rather than the observability argument in yesterday's
   handoff, which §4 contradicts.

Unchanged from yesterday: `LIDAR_SPIN_HZ = 5.5` is still unverified against the
A1's configuration, adversarial layouts are still unbuilt, and there is no
hardware pose reference, so nothing here is a sim-to-real result.

Test suite: 268 passed, 2 skipped (256 before today; 12 new covering derotation
geometry, the difference channel, and the gate gradient).
